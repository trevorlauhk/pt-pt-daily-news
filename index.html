So, he decides to go to the bank near his house. On Monday morning, João wakes up early. He has breakfast and puts on his best shirt. Before leaving, his mother says, "Take your ID card and tax number, son." At the bank, there are many people. João waits for fifteen minutes and then speaks to the clerk. "Good morning! I want to open an account, please," he says. The clerk smiles and replies, "Of course! I need some documents." João hands over his ID card, tax number, and proof of address. The clerk fills out the papers and explains, "This is your current account." João asks, "Can I deposit money today?" The clerk says yes. João returns home. His mother asks, "Did everything go well?" "Yes! Now I have a bank account and will receive my salary like an adult!"

English

In recent years, Portugal has stood out as one of Europe's most committed countries to the energy transition. Despite still facing challenges, such as dependence on outdated infrastructure and the need for significant investments, the potential of renewable energy in our country is undeniable. The sun, which bathes our coasts for more than 300 days a year, and the wind, which blows strongly in mountainous regions, are resources that, if well harnessed, could transform Portugal into a green powerhouse. However, for this to happen, a clear and coordinated strategy between government, businesses, and citizens is essential. One of the greatest advantages of renewable energy is its ability to reduce the carbon footprint, something that, in an era of climate change, is becoming increasingly urgent. Nevertheless, many argue that the intermittency of these sources — that is, the fact that the sun doesn’t always shine and the wind doesn’t always blow — represents an obstacle. Therefore, it is crucial to invest in storage solutions, such as high-capacity batteries, and smart grids that allow for efficient energy distribution. Moreover, for the population to embrace this change, fiscal incentives and awareness campaigns are needed to highlight the long-term benefits. Portugal has already taken important steps, such as closing coal plants and increasing wind and solar capacity. However, the road ahead is still long. Since technology is advancing at a rapid pace, it is possible that, in the not-too-distant future, renewable energies will be not only cheaper but also more reliable than traditional sources. It is up to us, therefore, to continue investing in this sector so that future generations inherit a more sustainable and energy-independent country.
