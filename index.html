English

Almost fifty years ago, Portugal woke up to a new dawn. In the early hours of April 25, 1974, the military from the Armed Forces Movement (MFA) took to the streets of Lisbon, not to wage war, but to end a dictatorship that had lasted nearly half a century. Although many citizens felt fear upon hearing the first radio announcements, the truth is that the revolution happened surprisingly peacefully. People came out of their homes with carnations in hand, offering them to the soldiers, and consequently, the day became known as the "Carnation Revolution". However, not everything was simple. Since the previous regime controlled all means of communication, it was necessary for the population to trust the military so that the democratic transition could occur without bloodshed. Nevertheless, the challenges did not end with the fall of the government. Portugal faced years of political and economic instability, but despite the difficulties, the country managed to rebuild itself. Today, April 25 is celebrated as a symbol of freedom and hope. It is important that new generations understand the meaning of this day so that the values of democracy and social justice continue to be upheld. After all, freedom is not a given, but rather an achievement that must be protected every day.
