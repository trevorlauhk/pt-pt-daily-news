Yesterday, Pedro decided to go to the hairdresser. He hadn’t cut his hair for three months, and it was already very long. Before leaving, he looked in the mirror and thought: "I need a modern haircut!" When he got to the street, he remembered he didn’t know where the salon was. So, he asked a lady: "Excuse me, do you know where there’s a hairdresser around here?" She smiled and replied: "Of course! Just go straight ahead and turn right at the second street." Pedro thanked her and went there. At the salon, a friendly hairdresser asked: "How do you want the cut?" He showed a photo on his phone and said: "I want it like this, but a little shorter." After half an hour, Pedro looked in the mirror and was happy. "It’s perfect!", he exclaimed. He paid and left a tip because he really liked the service. On his way home, he met his friend João. "Wow, Pedro! Your hair looks fantastic!", said João. Pedro smiled and replied: "Thanks! I’ll come back in a month."

English

In recent years, Portugal has become one of the most sought-after tourist destinations in Europe. While tourism brings clear economic benefits, such as job creation and GDP growth, some question whether the social and environmental costs are becoming too high. It is undeniable that without tourism, many regions of the country would face even greater difficulties; however, the excess of visitors has created problems that cannot be ignored. In cities like Lisbon and Porto, the rising cost of living is a reality that particularly affects local residents. Many young people and families are forced to move to peripheral areas, since housing prices have become unaffordable. Furthermore, mass tourism has led to a loss of cultural identity in some historic areas, where traditional shops are replaced by establishments catering only to tourists. Therefore, it is crucial to find balanced solutions so that tourism remains a source of wealth without destroying what makes Portugal unique. However, not all is bad news. Tourism has also enabled the preservation of monuments and the revitalization of degraded urban areas. As long as there is careful planning and effective public policies, it is possible to minimize the negative impacts. After all, the goal should not be to stop tourism but to manage it sustainably, so that future generations can also enjoy what our country has to offer.
