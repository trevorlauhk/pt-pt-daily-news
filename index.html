English

Yesterday morning, my car wouldn't start. I was very worried because I have to drive to work every day. So, I called my friend Miguel and he said he could take me to the neighborhood garage. We arrived at the garage at ten in the morning. The mechanic was a nice and older man. He looked at the engine and said the car had a serious battery problem. He was going to change the new battery this afternoon. I waited in the waiting room. I drank a hot coffee and read a book about trips in Portugal. After two hours, the mechanic said the car was finally ready. I was very happy because I needed the car for work. I paid one hundred euros with my credit card and left the garage. The car now works very well and makes little noise. Tomorrow I am going to take the car to work without problems.

Mais do que acolher: o desafio de construir uma sociedade verdadeiramente plural

B1-B2 (DEPLE/DIPLE)
🐶 Oreo Sir 小 Tips
Oreo Sir 汪汪！🍪 記住：「desde que」和「embora」後面一定要接接續式（conjuntivo），例如「desde que partilhe」、「embora existam」——就像我乖乖坐下等肉骨頭，條件滿足才搖尾巴！汪汪！🍪

English

There are those who say that Portugal is a country of emigrants, but they forget that, over the centuries, it has also welcomed many who came here seeking a better life and to fulfill their dreams. Today, immigration is once again at the center of public debate, and it is essential that we approach it with serenity and discernment. Although there are undeniable challenges — such as pressure on public services or the need to guarantee decent housing conditions — we cannot ignore that immigrants contribute decisively to the economy, to the cultural richness of the country and to social cohesion. It is important that we recognize their role, since without them many essential sectors would stop functioning. Integration, however, should not be confused with assimilation. For a society to be truly multicultural, it is necessary that we respect cultural differences harmoniously, provided that these do not violate the fundamental principles of human rights. In other words, we defend a society where everyone can maintain their identity, as long as they share the democratic values that unite us. Despite some alarmist discourses circulating on social media, the truth is that diversity has been, throughout history, a source of innovation and progress. Therefore, instead of fearing the other and closing ourselves off, we should ask ourselves what kind of society we want to build. Do we want a closed society, where fear prevails, or an open society, capable of learning from those who arrive? In order that we may make the most of this such complex reality, it is essential that we invest in intercultural education and in creating spaces for dialogue. Only then will we be able to transform immigration into a true enrichment for everyone and for the country as a whole.
