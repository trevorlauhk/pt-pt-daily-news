English

Yesterday, Ana needed to go to the post office. She wanted to send a package to her grandmother, who lives in Porto. Before leaving, Ana looked up her grandmother’s address and wrote it on a piece of paper. Then, she put the package in a box and sealed it with tape. When she arrived at the post office, there were many people in line. Ana waited for ten minutes, but she didn’t mind because she brought a book to read. Then, it was her turn. The clerk asked, "Good afternoon! How can I help you?" Ana replied, "I’d like to send this package to Porto, please." The clerk weighed the box and said, "That’ll be five euros and fifty cents." Ana paid and received a receipt. Before leaving, she asked, "How long will it take to arrive?" The clerk explained, "It usually arrives in two or three days." Ana left happy. Now, she’ll wait for her grandmother’s reply. Maybe she’ll call tomorrow to say thank you!

English

Some say the city never sleeps, and perhaps that’s precisely why so many dream of living there. The illuminated skyscrapers, the constant hustle and bustle of the streets, and the feeling that everything is within reach attract thousands of people every year. However, despite all the conveniences urban life offers, some prefer the silence of the mountains or the slow pace of a village, where time seems to pass differently. After all, what makes us choose between these two such distinct worlds? Living in the city has undeniable advantages. There are more job opportunities, an almost unlimited cultural scene, and services that, at first glance, make life more practical. Yet, this convenience often comes at a high price: stress, pollution, and loneliness, even in the midst of a crowd. It’s curious how, despite being surrounded by people, we can feel lonelier than ever. Consequently, it’s no surprise that many city dwellers dream of escaping to the countryside so they can catch their breath. On the other hand, rural life has a charm that the city can hardly match. Contact with nature, clean air, and the simplicity of daily life are aspects many value. However, it’s not all roses. The lack of infrastructure, the distance from essential services, and sometimes isolation can make rural life challenging. Still, for those who grew up among fields and streams, it’s hard to imagine living anywhere else but where their roots run deep. One thing is certain: whether in the city or the countryside, what matters is that each person finds their own balance. Perhaps the secret lies in not idealizing either side too much, since both have their virtues and flaws. What truly counts is that, wherever we are, we feel that life makes sense and that our heart beats to the right rhythm.
