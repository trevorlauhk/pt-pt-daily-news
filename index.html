English

Yesterday, Maria left home to buy a birthday cake. She wanted to go to the bakery "Doce Sabor," but she didn’t know the way. So, she decided to ask for directions on the street. First, she saw a kind elderly man walking slowly. Maria approached him and asked, "Excuse me, do you know where the bakery 'Doce Sabor' is?" The man smiled and answered, "Yes, it’s near here." Go straight to the traffic light, then turn right. The bakery is next to the bank. Maria thanked him and followed the instructions. But after turning right, she didn’t see the bank or the bakery. She felt a little confused. Then, she saw a girl with a dog. She asked again, "Excuse me, is the bakery 'Doce Sabor' near here?" The girl said, "Ah, yes! But the man was wrong." In the end, she thought, "Next time, I’ll ask two people!"

Turismo em Portugal: Bênção ou Maldição para as Cidades Históricas?

English

In recent years, Portugal has witnessed exponential growth in tourism, a phenomenon that, while bringing clear economic benefits, raises concerning questions about the sustainability of our historic cities. Lisbon and Porto, for example, have become top destinations for millions of visitors, who, although they contribute to the dynamism of the local economy, also exert increasing pressure on already fragile infrastructures. It is undeniable that tourism generates jobs and wealth; however, we cannot ignore the negative effects this boom brings, such as real estate speculation and gentrification, which push local residents out of urban centers. Therefore, it is urgent to reflect on how to balance tourism development with the preservation of cultural identity. Many voices argue that stricter measures should be implemented so that tourism does not become a threat to the quality of life of the Portuguese. Since the country opened its doors to the world, we have seen entire neighborhoods transform into commercial zones aimed only at tourists, losing the authenticity that once made them unique. Nevertheless, some argue that with intelligent management, it is possible to reconcile sector growth with heritage protection. One thing is certain: if we do not act now, we risk seeing our historic cities emptied of meaning. Thus, it is essential that authorities, businesses, and the general population work together, so that Portugal remains a welcoming destination without losing its soul.
