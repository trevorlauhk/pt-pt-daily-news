English

João woke up early on Saturday. He wanted to go to Lisbon to visit his grandmother. Before leaving, he had breakfast and drank a coffee. Then, he grabbed his backpack and went to the train station. At the station, there were many people. João got a little nervous. He looked for the ticket office but didn’t find it right away. So, he asked a lady: "Excuse me, where can I buy tickets?" The lady smiled and said: "It’s over there, next to that red machine." João thanked her and went there. At the ticket office, he said: "I’d like a ticket to Lisbon, please." The clerk asked: "Round trip or one way?" He paid with his card and got the ticket. Then, he looked at the clock and saw it was 9:15. He ran to the platform, but the train had already left. João sighed and thought: "Next time, I’ll arrive earlier."

English

In recent years, the idea of a four-day workweek has gained traction in several countries, including Portugal. However, although some studies suggest this change could boost productivity and worker well-being, there are those who argue it would bring more challenges than benefits. Therefore, it is essential to analyze both sides of this debate before it becomes a widespread reality. On one hand, supporters of reducing the workweek claim that, since employees would have more time to rest, their motivation and efficiency would improve significantly. Companies that have already adopted this model, such as some in the UK and Iceland, have reported not only a decrease in stress but also greater talent retention. For this trend to continue, it is crucial that governments and businesses invest in flexible policies and adapt their processes. However, critics warn that not all sectors could implement this change without losses. For example, areas like healthcare or hospitality, which rely on extended hours, could face logistical difficulties. Furthermore, there is a fear that, even though workers would have more free time, salaries might not adjust to this reduction in hours, worsening social inequalities. As long as this model is applied unevenly, the risk of polarization in the job market will increase. In conclusion, while the four-day week may seem like an ideal solution to improve quality of life, it is necessary to weigh its long-term impacts. If, by chance, this measure is adopted, it should be accompanied by clear regulations so it does not become a source of new problems. After all, the goal is not just to work less, but to live better.
