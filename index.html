English

Mrs. Ana works in a small shop in the city center. Every day, she opens the shop at eight in the morning and closes at seven in the evening. She really likes her job, but one day, she had a problem: the shop door broke and wouldn’t close properly. So, she thought: "I need insurance for the shop!". The next day, Mrs. Ana went to an insurance company. There, a kind lady explained everything. "This insurance covers theft, fire, and damage to the shop," she said. Mrs. Ana was happy but asked: "How much does it cost?". The lady replied: "It costs fifty euros a month. It’s a good price, isn’t it?". Mrs. Ana thought for a moment. She didn’t have much money, but she knew insurance was important. So, she decided to take it. "I’ll pay now," she said. Then, she signed the papers and received the policy. A week later, a man tried to rob the shop. Fortunately, the insurance covered everything. Mrs. Ana was relieved and said: "How good that I took out insurance!". Now, she sleeps peacefully every night.

English

In recent years, Portugal has become one of the most sought-after tourist destinations in Europe. Cities like Lisbon and Porto are filled with visitors, souvenir shops multiply, and hotels appear on every corner. However, despite the clear economic benefits that tourism brings, are we paying too high a price? Since the number of tourists keeps growing, many residents complain about rising house prices, the loss of identity in historic neighborhoods, and even the difficulty in finding basic products in local shops. Therefore, it is crucial that the government implements policies to balance tourism growth with the quality of life for the Portuguese. So that future generations can enjoy what remains of cultural authenticity, action must be taken now. Nevertheless, we cannot ignore the positive aspects. Tourism creates jobs, boosts the economy, and promotes cultural exchange. As long as there is careful planning, it is possible for everyone to benefit. Thus, the challenge is not to ban tourism but to manage it sustainably, so that Portugal remains a welcoming country without losing its soul.
