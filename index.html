Then, she saw a long queue at the ticket office and got nervous. A kind gentleman asked, "Do you need help?" Ana replied, "Yes, please! It’s my first time traveling alone." The man explained, "First, choose your destination. Then, say the time. "How beautiful!" she thought. After forty minutes, she arrived in Sintra. Ana was very happy because now she knew how to buy tickets by herself!

English

When we think of Portuguese gastronomy, it’s almost impossible not to salivate at the memory of the aroma of grilled cod or the rich flavor of a good *cozido à portuguesa*. However, what many don’t realize is that these dishes, which we now consider traditional, are the result of centuries of influences, adaptations, and, above all, resilience. Therefore, talking about the evolution of gastronomy in Portugal is, in many ways, talking about the history of the Portuguese people themselves. Despite globalization bringing new ingredients and techniques, the truth is that Portuguese cuisine never lost its essence. On the contrary, it knew how to incorporate the new without forgetting the old. For example, the famous *pastel de nata*, which is now a national symbol, was born in convent ovens in the 18th century when nuns sought a way to use the leftover egg whites after using the yolks to starch clothes. However, it was only in the 20th century that this pastry became popular, thanks to *Pastéis de Belém*, which kept the original recipe so that the flavor would not be lost. Once tourism began to grow, the *pastel de nata* became an ambassador of Portuguese culture, proving that tradition can indeed be innovative. Today, Portuguese chefs like José Avillez or Kiko Martins are revolutionizing national gastronomy by blending modern techniques with local products. It’s fascinating to see how, despite using contemporary methods, these professionals never fail to honor their roots. Consequently, Portuguese cuisine continues to be a mirror of the country’s identity: diverse, creative, and deeply connected to the land. As long as there is respect for the past, the future of gastronomy in Portugal seems brighter than ever.
