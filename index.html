English

In recent years, social media has become the main stage for public debate. However, this freedom that many celebrate has brought with it a growing problem: disinformation. While the internet allows anyone to share their opinions, not everything published is true—and sometimes, it’s not even harmless. It is crucial, therefore, to reflect on how far freedom of expression should be protected when what’s at stake is the manipulation of reality. Since digital platforms have no borders, the spread of fake news can have global consequences. Imagine, for example, a rumor about a health crisis spreading in seconds: even if authorities try to debunk it, the damage will already be done. Nevertheless, banning certain content raises another delicate question: who decides what is true? In order to avoid arbitrary censorship, a balance must be found, perhaps through transparent regulations and user education. Some argue that as long as there is freedom, people will learn to distinguish right from wrong. However, reality shows this isn’t always the case. Often, emotions speak louder than reason, and a lie repeated enough times can become ‘truth’ for those who hear it. Consequently, it is essential that social media platforms take greater responsibility—not just by deleting content, but also by promoting reliable sources. In short, freedom of expression should not be a shield for lies. It is possible—and necessary—to defend both values: the free circulation of ideas and protection against disinformation. After all, an informed society is a stronger society.
