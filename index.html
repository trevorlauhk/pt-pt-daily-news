Mrs. Rosa lives alone in an old building in Porto. Last night, she heard strange noises in the street. It was ten o'clock, and she was already in bed. At first, she thought it was the wind, but then she heard loud voices and breaking glass. Mrs. Rosa got scared. She didn’t know what to do. Then, she remembered the police number: 112. She picked up the phone, but her hands were shaking a lot. She took a deep breath and dialed the number. A calm voice answered: "Police, how can I help you?". Mrs. Rosa explained what she heard. The police said they would send a car to check. After hanging up, Mrs. Rosa locked the door and waited. Ten minutes later, she heard sirens. She looked out the window and saw two police officers talking to a man in the street. The next day, Mrs. Rosa told her son what happened. He was proud of her and said: "Mom, you did the right thing by calling the police!". Now, Mrs. Rosa knows that if she needs help, she can ask for it.

English

For years, the housing crisis in Lisbon and Porto has been discussed, yet few seem to have a concrete solution to this problem affecting thousands of families. Despite the government implementing some measures, such as increasing taxes on empty houses, the truth is that prices continue to rise, making it nearly impossible for young people to buy or even rent a decent apartment. It is urgent that a solution be found so that cities do not turn into museums for tourists, where only the wealthiest can live. One of the main reasons for this crisis is mass tourism. Since Lisbon and Porto became trendy destinations, foreign investors have bought countless properties to turn them into short-term rentals. Consequently, many residents have been forced out of historic centers, as landlords prefer to rent to tourists, who pay more and for shorter periods. However, not all is lost. Some believe that if municipalities better regulate the market, it will still be possible to balance tourism with housing. Another worrying factor is real estate speculation. Often, buildings are left abandoned for years until prices rise enough to make selling them worthwhile. Although there are laws penalizing owners who do not maintain their properties, these are not rigorously enforced. Therefore, it is crucial that authorities act more firmly so that young people do not have to emigrate in search of a more affordable life. In conclusion, the housing crisis is not a simple problem, but it requires immediate action. If nothing is done, we risk losing the soul of our cities, where only the most fortunate will have a place.
