English

Yesterday was a special day for Pedro. He got his driver’s license two weeks ago and now has his first car! It’s a small red car, but he really likes it. This morning, Pedro looked at the fuel gauge and saw it was almost empty. "I need to fill up," he thought. So, he grabbed his keys, phone, and wallet and left the house. The gas station is near his home, but Pedro had never filled up by himself before. When he arrived, he saw different types of fuel: regular gasoline, premium gasoline, and diesel. A kind man next to him helped. He opened the fuel tank cap, took the nozzle, and chose regular gasoline. He filled the tank slowly and stopped when it was full. He paid with his card at the pump and got the receipt. When he returned to the car, he felt very proud. "Now I know how to fill up by myself!" he said out loud. Before driving off, he checked the oil and water, just like his dad taught him.

English

In recent years, social media has become an unavoidable part of young people's lives. While it offers incredible opportunities for connection and learning, many experts warn about the risks these platforms may pose. It's undeniable that through Instagram or TikTok, teenagers discover new cultures, develop creative skills, and even find support in online communities. However, the pressure to fit into unrealistic standards of beauty or success can have serious consequences on their self-esteem. Since algorithms prioritize content that generates engagement, young people are frequently exposed to unattainable ideals. Consequently, many feel inadequate when comparing their real lives to the edited versions they see online. Nevertheless, not all is lost. As long as there is solid digital education, both in schools and at home, young people can learn to use these tools critically and healthily. So that this generation does not fall into the trap of external validation, it is crucial that parents and educators promote open conversations about the dangers of social media. After all, the goal should not be to prohibit, but to teach how to navigate this digital world with balance. May young people see social media as a mirror that reflects their potential, not as a prison that limits their freedom to be who they are.
