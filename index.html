English

Yesterday, Pedro went to the bank to open his first account. He was nervous, but also very happy. "Now I'm an adult," he thought. Before leaving home, Pedro checked his backpack. He had his ID card, tax number, and proof of address. At the bank, a kind lady called him by his name. "Good morning, Pedro! Please, have a seat." She explained everything calmly. First, he filled out a form. Then, he signed some papers. The lady also asked if he wanted a debit card. "Of course!", Pedro replied. While waiting, Pedro remembered his first salary. He would receive the money next week. When he finished, the lady gave him a brochure with the account rules. Pedro thanked her and left the bank with a smile.

English

In recent years, Artificial Intelligence (AI) has taken up an increasingly large space in our lives. From virtual assistants that help us organize our daily routines to algorithms capable of diagnosing diseases with precision, it is undeniable that this technology is here to stay. However, despite all the advantages it offers, some fear that AI might one day replace human labor or, even worse, escape our control. On one hand, AI allows us to save time and resources by automating repetitive tasks and freeing us for more creative activities. For example, companies that implement AI systems can increase their productivity since these tools work without breaks and with minimal error margins. Moreover, in areas like medicine, AI has been a valuable ally, helping professionals detect health issues that might otherwise go unnoticed. Nevertheless, it is important that we do not let enthusiasm blind us. Even though AI is a powerful tool, we must not forget that it was created by humans and, therefore, is subject to flaws and biases. Some argue that if we are not careful, we could create systems that reinforce social inequalities or make decisions without transparency. Consequently, it is essential that clear regulations exist so that this technology is used ethically and responsibly. In conclusion, AI is neither an absolute blessing nor a curse. Everything depends on how we use it. As long as we are aware of its limits and potential risks, we can enjoy its benefits without compromising our future. After all, technology itself is neither good nor bad—it is we who give it meaning.
