Yesterday, Sofia decided to send a package to her grandmother. She lives in Porto, but her grandmother lives in Faro, in the Algarve. Sofia wanted to send some typical sweets from the region and a warm sweater because winter is coming. First, she went to the supermarket and bought a *bolo-rei* and two cans of almonds. Then, she wrapped everything in gift paper and put it in a cardboard box. Before closing the box, she wrote a nice letter to her grandmother. At the post office, Sofia spoke to the clerk. "Good morning! I’d like to send this package to Faro, please," she said. The clerk weighed the box and asked, "Do you prefer regular or express mail?" Sofia thought for a moment and answered, "Regular, please. It’s not urgent." The clerk calculated the price and said, "That’ll be eight euros and fifty cents." Sofia paid and received a receipt. When she got home, she called her grandmother to let her know. "Grandma, I sent you a package! I hope you like the sweets," she said. Her grandmother was very happy and replied, "Thank you, my dear! I’ll be waiting eagerly."

English

In recent years, Artificial Intelligence (AI) has transformed the world in ways that, just a few decades ago, would have seemed like science fiction. From virtual assistants that answer our questions to algorithms capable of diagnosing diseases with precision, AI has become a constant presence in our lives. However, despite all the advantages it offers, it is crucial that we reflect on the challenges this technology brings, so that we can use it ethically and responsibly. On one hand, AI has the potential to revolutionize sectors such as medicine, education, and even art. Imagine, for example, a doctor who, with the help of an intelligent system, can detect a tumor in its early stages, saving thousands of lives. Yet, there are those who fear that as these technologies become more advanced, they may replace human jobs, creating social inequalities. It is therefore important that governments and companies implement policies to ensure that no one is left behind in this technological race. Moreover, complex ethical questions arise. Should we allow machines to make decisions that affect human lives, such as in the case of autonomous cars? And how can we ensure that algorithms do not reproduce existing biases in society? Although AI is a powerful tool, we must not forget that it is programmed by humans and, consequently, reflects our own limitations and flaws. In short, Artificial Intelligence is neither inherently good nor bad: it all depends on how we use it. It is up to us, as a society, to ensure that this technology is used for the common good, provided that we do not lose sight of the human values that define us. After all, no matter how intelligent a machine may be, it can never replace the empathy, creativity, and ability to dream that make us truly human.
