English

In the 21st century, we are witnessing a frantic race to modernize cities, where skyscrapers and shopping centers sprout like mushrooms after the rain. However, this rapid evolution comes at a price: the irreparable loss of our historical and architectural heritage. Although many argue that preservation is costly and unnecessary, it is crucial that we recognize the intangible value these monuments hold. It is not just about stones or bricks, but about living testimonies of a collective identity that, once lost, can never be recovered. Imagine walking through the streets of Lisbon or Porto and coming across a centuries-old building, whose walls tell stories of past generations. It is undeniable that these spaces connect us to the past, allowing us to understand who we are and where we come from. Nevertheless, real estate pressure and the lack of sensitivity from some political decision-makers threaten to erase these memories. Consequently, it is urgent that there be greater awareness of the importance of protecting these treasures, so that future generations may also enjoy them. Moreover, heritage is not just a legacy of the past, but also an economic driver. Cities like Évora or Sintra, which have preserved their historic centers, attract millions of tourists every year, generating jobs and wealth. Therefore, investing in conservation is not a luxury, but a smart strategy, provided it is done with criteria and respect for cultural value. In short, preserving heritage is preserving our soul as a people. Let us not regret, in the future, having let disappear what defines us.
