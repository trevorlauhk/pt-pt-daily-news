English

Yesterday, Ana went to the post office because she needed to send a package to her mother. Her mother lives in Porto and has a birthday tomorrow. Ana bought a nice book and a box of chocolates. Before going, she wrapped everything in colorful paper. When she arrived at the post office, there were many people in line. Ana waited for ten minutes, and then it was her turn. The clerk was friendly and asked, "Good afternoon! What would you like to send?" Ana replied, "I want to send this package to Porto, please." The clerk weighed the box and said, "It will cost five euros." Ana paid and received a receipt. Then, the clerk gave her a tracking number. Ana was happy because now her mother will receive the gift on time. Before leaving, she also bought a stamp for a letter. In the end, she thought, "Next time, I’ll bring fewer things in my bag because the line is long!"

English

Almost fifty years ago, Portugal woke up different. It wasn’t an ordinary awakening, but rather the beginning of a new era, marked by the sound of revolutionary songs and the fresh scent of red carnations. The 25th of April, 1974, wasn’t just a military coup; it was, above all, a peaceful revolution that restored freedom to a people who had lived for decades under the shadow of an oppressive dictatorship. Even though many young people didn’t live through that moment, it’s essential that they know about it, since it shaped the country we inhabit today. The so-called "Carnation Revolution" showed the world that change can happen without violence, as long as there is courage and unity. However, not everything was simple. In the years that followed, Portugal faced enormous challenges, such as decolonization and political instability, which demanded sacrifices and patience. Today, when we look back, we realize that the 25th of April wasn’t just a day, but a long and complex process. It’s important that new generations understand that democracy isn’t a given, but rather an achievement that must be protected. Therefore, celebrating this date isn’t just about remembering the past, but also reaffirming our commitment to a fairer and freer future. After all, as the poet said, "freedom is like air: we only notice it when it’s missing." May we never lack it, so that Portugal remains a beacon of hope.
