I looked at everything again and said, "I’d like a custard tart, please." My sister laughed and said, "Always the same!" Then, we sat at a small table. I tried the custard tart, and it was warm and sweet. My sister ate the chocolate cake and said it was the best she’d ever tasted. Before leaving, we bought two loaves of bread for dinner. Portugal Multicultural: Um Mosaico que Ainda Precisa de Cimento

B1-B2 (DEPLE/DIPLE)
🐶 Oreo Sir 小 Tips
汪汪！各位港漂同學們 🍪，我是你們的Oreo Sir！今天來聊聊這篇超有深度的文章～文中用了好多高級連接詞（像是「a fim de que」、「desde que」）和虛擬式（conjuntivo），讓句子變得又流暢又專業！記得：當你想表達「希望」、「目的」或「條件」時，就要請出conjuntivo這位大明星🌟。比如「É importante que tu *estejas* aberto a novas culturas」（重要的是你要對新文化持開放態度）。多練習這些連接詞，你的文章就會像Oreo Sir的毛一樣柔順有光澤～加油，未來的多元文化大使！🐾🍪

English

In recent years, Portugal has seen a significant increase in immigration—a phenomenon that, despite its challenges, deeply enriches our society. However, for this diversity to truly become a cultural mosaic, a joint effort to promote integration is essential. It is not enough for immigrants to learn the language or understand Portuguese traditions; it is equally important that local citizens be willing to welcome and understand differences, so that everyone may feel part of the same community. Many argue that immigration can overwhelm public services, such as healthcare or education. Nevertheless, recent studies show that, as long as there are adequate support policies, immigrants make a positive contribution to the economy and to the country’s demographic renewal. Therefore, instead of viewing this phenomenon with fear, we should see it as an opportunity to grow as a nation. Of course, integration is not a simple process. There are linguistic, cultural, and even prejudicial barriers that need to be overcome. Although some progress has been made—such as Portuguese language courses for foreigners and multicultural events—there is still much to be done. For instance, it would be desirable for companies to adopt more inclusive measures, so that immigrants could access qualified jobs, regardless of their origin. In short, Portugal has everything it takes to become a model of a multicultural society. Since diversity is already a reality, it is up to us to decide whether we want it to be a source of division or unity. May this be the moment when, together, we build bridges instead of walls.
