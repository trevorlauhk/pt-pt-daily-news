English

Mrs. Ana lives alone in a small apartment in Lisbon. She loves her home, but last night she had a problem: the kitchen faucet broke, and water started flooding the floor. She tried to turn it off but couldn’t. So, she called Mr. João, her neighbor from the first floor. He came quickly and helped her shut off the water. "Mrs. Ana, you need home insurance." "If you have insurance, you won’t have to pay for everything yourself." Mrs. Ana thought: "He’s right. I’m old and don’t have much money." The next day, she went to an insurance agency near her home. "This insurance covers fires, floods, and thefts. It also helps if you need repairs." "How much does it cost?" Mrs. Ana asked. "Only twenty euros a month," the lady replied. Mrs. Ana thought it was cheap and signed the contract. Now, she sleeps more peacefully. If another problem happens, the insurance will help. She’s also going to tell her friend Maria because Maria doesn’t have insurance either.

English

In recent years, the internet has become the main stage for public debate. While this revolution has democratized access to information, it has also opened the door to a worrying phenomenon: disinformation. The question is clear—how can we balance freedom of expression, a fundamental right, with the need to combat fake news that can destabilize societies? Although many argue that any restriction on freedom of expression is dangerous, it is undeniable that the spread of online lies has real consequences. We only need to recall elections influenced by *fake news* or anti-vaccine movements that put lives at risk. Therefore, it is crucial that digital platforms take responsibility, even if that means moderating content. However, who decides what is true or false? Since the line between opinion and disinformation is thin, any censorship must be applied with extreme caution so that it does not turn into an abuse of power. Nevertheless, the solution does not lie solely in laws or algorithms. It is essential that citizens develop critical thinking, questioning sources and verifying facts before sharing information. After all, freedom of expression should not be a shield for irresponsibility. As long as there is transparency and education, perhaps we can navigate this digital labyrinth without sacrificing the values that define us. Is the balance achieved? Not yet, but dialogue is the first step.
