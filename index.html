So, she decided to start with the fruit. She picked apples and bananas, but she couldn’t find any oranges. "What a shame!" she thought. Then, she went to the dairy section. The milk was on sale, and she took two cartons. Before paying, she remembered she also needed soap. She ran to the hygiene section and chose a fragrant one. At the checkout, the line was shorter. English

In recent years, social media has become an unavoidable part of young people’s lives. While it facilitates communication and allows almost instant access to information, many experts wonder whether these benefits might, however, hide negative consequences. Since teenagers spend hours scrolling through idealized profiles, it’s natural that they develop a distorted view of reality. Consequently, problems such as anxiety, low self-esteem, and even depression have been on the rise, especially among those who feel their lives don’t measure up to the unrealistic standards they see online. Yet, not everything is negative. Social media also offers unique opportunities, such as the ability to create support communities, share interests, and even mobilize social causes. As long as they are used in moderation and with a critical mindset, they can be a powerful tool. So that young people can make the most of these platforms, it’s crucial that parents and educators guide them, teaching them to distinguish between what is authentic and what is mere illusion. Therefore, it’s up to all of us to reflect: are social media a mirror that reflects who we are, or a prison that prevents us from living fully? The answer, as almost always, lies in balance. After all, technology is neither good nor bad in itself; everything depends on how we use it.
