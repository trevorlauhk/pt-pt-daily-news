English

Yesterday, Maria decided to send a package to her grandmother. Her grandmother lives in Porto, and Maria lives in Lisbon. She wanted to send some traditional sweets and a warm sweater because winter is coming. First, Maria went to the supermarket and bought a *bolo-rei* and two *pastéis de nata*. Then, she went to the clothing store and picked a blue sweater, her grandmother’s favorite color. When she got home, she wrapped everything with colorful paper and ribbon. This morning, Maria woke up early. She had breakfast and then went to the post office. In line, she talked to a kind lady who was also sending a package. The lady said, "The post office is always busy, but the service is good." When it was Maria’s turn, she handed the package to the clerk. The clerk weighed the box and said, "It will cost eight euros." Maria paid and received the receipt. Now, the package will arrive in Porto in two days. Maria is happy because her grandmother will love the gift!

English

In recent years, Lisbon and Porto have witnessed a worrying rise in housing prices, a problem that particularly affects young people and middle-income families. Although the government has implemented some measures, such as the «Porta 65» program, many feel these are not enough to solve a crisis that seems endless. It is undeniable that demand for housing in these cities has increased, since tourism and foreign investment have grown exponentially. However, supply has not kept up with this demand, resulting in unprecedented real estate speculation. Young people, who dream of living independently, are forced to postpone plans or share housing in often precarious conditions. Some argue that the solution would involve limiting short-term rentals, so that more properties could be made available for long-term leasing. Nevertheless, this measure could have negative consequences for the tourism sector, which is vital for the national economy. Therefore, it is necessary to find a balance that benefits both residents and visitors. Furthermore, it is crucial to invest in social housing and create tax incentives for landlords who offer affordable rents. Only then can we ensure that cities remain spaces of opportunity, rather than exclusion. After all, a city without young people is a city without a future. And Lisbon and Porto deserve a better future.
