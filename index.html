English

Sofia woke up with a toothache. She didn’t like going to the dentist, but she knew she needed to make an appointment. So, she picked up her phone and called the clinic. — Good morning! I’d like to book an appointment with Dr. Mendes, please — Sofia said. — Of course! You’re available tomorrow at 10 AM or Thursday at 3 PM — the receptionist replied. — Tomorrow at 10 AM is perfect. Thank you! — Sofia said, relieved. The next day, Sofia arrived at the clinic a little nervous. The receptionist smiled and said: — You can sit over there. The doctor will be right with you. After ten minutes, Dr. Mendes called her: — Sofia, you can come in now. The dentist examined her tooth and said: — It’s not serious, but you need a small filling. Sofia took a deep breath and said: — Alright, doctor. I trust you. After the appointment, Sofia left the clinic feeling a huge sense of relief. On her way home, she thought: "Next time, I’ll go to the dentist before I feel pain!"

Cidades que Andam: O Futuro da Mobilidade Urbana Começa nos Autocarros e Metro

B1-B2 (DEPLE/DIPLE)
🐶 Oreo Sir 小 Tips
汪汪！Oreo Sir 來啦！🍪 親愛的港漂同學們，今天的文章充滿了「a fim de que」和「desde que」這些超酷的連接詞，它們可是讓你的葡語寫作瞬間升級的秘密武器！記住，這些詞後面通常跟著「conjuntivo」（虛擬式），比如「a fim de que as cidades se tornem mais sustentáveis」。別怕用錯，多練習就能像我一樣自信地「汪」出完美句子！🐾🍪 來，咬一口餅乾，繼續加油吧！

English

We live in an era where cities are growing at a rapid pace, and with them, the challenges of urban mobility. Although many believe that the private car is synonymous with freedom, the truth is that in congested urban centers, it can become a real prison on wheels. Therefore, it is urgent to rethink how we move around so that our cities become more sustainable and livable. Public transport, however, does not always receive the recognition it deserves. Since it is shared by thousands of people daily, it should be seen as the backbone of urban mobility. Nevertheless, there are still those who consider it slow or unreliable, despite studies showing that, in many European cities, the metro and buses are faster than cars during rush hour. Moreover, if we all chose to leave the car at home, the benefits would be immediate: less pollution, less noise, and, of course, less stress. It is true that not all cities are prepared for this change. There are areas where the public transport network is scarce, forcing citizens to rely on cars. However, this does not mean we should give up. On the contrary, it is essential that governments invest in infrastructure so that no one is left behind. As long as there is political will and careful planning, it is possible to create efficient systems that serve the entire population. Finally, urban mobility is not just a matter of logistics, but also of mindset. If each of us makes an effort to use public transport more, we will be contributing to a better future. After all, a city that moves is a city that lives—and breathes—with more health and harmony.
