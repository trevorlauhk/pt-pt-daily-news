Ana arrived in Lisbon two months ago. She works at a café in the city center and really likes life here. But Ana doesn’t want to live in a small room forever. So, she decides to look for an apartment to rent. On Saturday morning, Ana opens her computer and looks for ads. She sees many beautiful apartments, but most are expensive. Then, she finds a small but cheap apartment near her work. Ana calls the landlord and schedules a visit. At three in the afternoon, she goes to see the apartment. The landlord, Mr. Costa, is friendly and shows her everything. Ana loves it! Ana signs the contract and pays the deposit. On Sunday, she goes to buy some things for her new home. She’s very excited because, finally, she’s going to have her own place in Lisbon!

English

In the 21st century, education faces a fascinating dilemma: is it still the compass guiding new generations, or has it become a maze of uncertainties? While technological advances have revolutionized how we learn, many educators question whether the current system is truly prepared to shape critical and adaptable citizens. It is undeniable that the internet has democratized access to knowledge; however, the overload of information can sometimes confuse rather than clarify. Since the job market demands increasingly diverse skills, schools must rethink their methods. Memorizing facts is no longer enough; it is essential that students develop creative thinking and resilience. Consequently, many experts argue that education should focus less on standardized exams and more on interdisciplinary projects that encourage collaboration. Yet, this change will not happen overnight, especially in countries with limited resources. Ever since the pandemic accelerated the shift to distance learning, it has become clear that flexibility is crucial. In order for all students to have equal opportunities, investment in digital infrastructure and teacher training is necessary. After all, education should not be a privilege but a right accessible to everyone, regardless of their background or economic status. In short, the 21st century calls for an education that not only transmits knowledge but also inspires and prepares young people for the challenges of a constantly changing world. Let this, therefore, be our greatest commitment.
