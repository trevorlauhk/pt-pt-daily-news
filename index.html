Ana woke up with a very bad headache. She didn’t want to get out of bed, but she knew she needed help. So, she called her friend Maria and said, "Maria, I don’t feel well." "I think I’m going to the doctor." Maria replied, "I’ll go with you! I’ll wait for you at the health center door at nine." At nine o’clock, the two friends were in the doctor’s office. The doctor asked, "What’s wrong, Ana?" "I’ve had a headache since yesterday. I also feel tired and have a fever," Ana explained. The doctor examined her and said, "It’s not serious, but you need to rest." "I’ll prescribe some pills. Take two a day, after lunch and dinner." Ana thanked her and left the office. On the way home, she bought the medicine at the pharmacy. "Now I’ll go home and rest," she thought. "Tomorrow I’ll already feel better!"

English

In recent years, social media has become the main stage for public debate. However, this democratization of speech has brought with it a growing problem: disinformation. While some argue that freedom of expression should be absolute, others believe that since fake news can cause irreparable harm, limits must be imposed. But where do we draw the line between censorship and responsibility? Although the Constitution guarantees the right to free expression, this does not mean that all opinions deserve to be amplified without consequences. For example, when an influencer spreads conspiracy theories that endanger public health, are we not facing an abuse of that freedom? Yet, banning certain speeches raises ethical questions: who decides what is true or false? And how can we prevent governments or platforms from using this power to silence uncomfortable voices? Therefore, the solution is not just about regulation but also about education. In order for citizens to develop critical thinking, it is essential to invest in digital literacy. Moreover, platforms should be more transparent about their algorithms, which often prioritize sensationalism over truth. After all, freedom of expression is only truly free when used responsibly—and this is something we all must learn before lies become the new normal.
