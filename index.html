English

In recent years, Artificial Intelligence (AI) has been transforming our daily lives in ways that, a decade ago, would have seemed like science fiction. From virtual assistants that help us organize our day to algorithms capable of diagnosing diseases with precision, AI has become an unavoidable presence. However, despite all the advantages it offers, some fear that this technology may one day replace human labor or, worse still, slip out of our control. It is undeniable that AI allows us to perform tasks with impressive efficiency. For example, in fields like medicine, its ability to analyze large amounts of data in seconds can save lives. However, it is crucial that we do not let enthusiasm blind us and that we also consider the risks. Since AI relies on data to learn, there is a danger of perpetuating existing biases if the data used is not representative. Moreover, some argue that as machines become smarter, they may make decisions we do not understand or that are unethical. Therefore, the question is not whether we should use AI, but how to do so responsibly. In order for this technology to benefit society as a whole, there must be clear regulation, and developers must prioritize transparency. After all, technology should serve humanity, not the other way around. As long as ethical boundaries are established, AI can continue to be a valuable ally in human progress.
