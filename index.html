English

Yesterday, Ana and Pedro went to the zoo with their parents. They left home early because they wanted to see all the animals. When they arrived, the sun was shining and the birds were singing. First, they saw the lions. The lions were sleeping under a tree. Ana was a little scared, but Pedro laughed and said, "They won’t hurt you!" Then, they went to see the monkeys. The monkeys were jumping from branch to branch and eating bananas. Pedro also wanted to eat a banana, but his mom said, "Not now, after lunch." At noon, they had a picnic near the lake. They ate sandwiches, fruit, and drank juice. While they ate, the ducks swam in the lake and begged for food. Pedro gave them some bread. In the afternoon, they saw the elephants. The elephants were very big and had huge ears! Ana loved touching an elephant’s trunk. It was so funny! At the end of the day, they were tired but happy. Ana said, "I want to come back tomorrow!" But her dad replied, "Let’s rest first, then we’ll go again."

English

In recent years, cities have been growing at a rapid pace, and with this growth come ever-greater challenges. One of the most pressing is undoubtedly urban mobility. While many argue that private cars are the most convenient option, is it really sustainable for everyone to use their own vehicle? The answer seems obvious: no. However, the solution is not that simple. Public transport, though a more eco-friendly alternative, still faces many obstacles. On the one hand, it is essential that metro, bus, and train networks be efficient and cover all areas of the city. On the other, it is crucial that citizens trust the system, which does not always happen. Often, people choose cars because they fear public transport will not arrive on time or that there will be no available seats. Therefore, it is necessary to invest not only in expanding infrastructure but also in improving service quality. However, improving public transport is not enough if there is no shift in mindset. People need to understand that by using cars less, they are contributing to a healthier environment and less congested cities. Since we all share the same space, it is only fair that we all make an effort. For this change to happen, awareness campaigns and tax incentives can play a crucial role. In conclusion, public transport is undoubtedly a key piece in solving urban mobility problems. Despite the challenges that still need to be overcome, it is possible to create more sustainable and livable cities as long as there is political will and collaboration from citizens. After all, the future of our cities depends on the choices we make today.
