English

In recent years, the national healthcare system has faced challenges that, while not new, have become more evident with the aging population and increasing pressure on services. It is undeniable that Portugal has one of the best healthcare systems in Europe; however, the reality is that many citizens still face long waits for appointments or surgeries, which can consequently worsen health problems that could have been prevented with early diagnosis. One of the most concerning issues is the shortage of healthcare professionals in some regions, particularly in the country's interior. Although the government has implemented measures to attract doctors and nurses to these areas, such as financial incentives or better working conditions, it is crucial that these initiatives are sustainable in the long term. After all, it is not enough to just recruit; it is necessary to ensure that these professionals remain motivated and that local communities have continuous access to quality care. Additionally, the digitalization of healthcare services has been touted as a solution to improve efficiency, yet many users, especially the elderly, struggle to adapt to these changes. It is important that, as we move forward with technology, we do not leave behind those who need support the most. Therefore, training and support are essential so that everyone can benefit from innovations without feeling excluded. In short, the national healthcare system is a cornerstone of society, but for it to continue fulfilling its role, a joint effort between the government, healthcare professionals, and citizens is needed. Only then will it be possible to ensure that no one is left behind, regardless of their age or place of residence.
