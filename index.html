English

Mrs. Ana lives in a small apartment in Porto. Last night, she took a shower before bed. Suddenly, the water turned cold! She tried turning the tap more, but it didn’t help. "How annoying!" she thought. This morning, Mrs. Ana called her daughter. "Daughter, what should I do? The heater is broken." Her daughter replied, "Mom, you need to call a plumber." Then, she called Mr. Silva. "Good afternoon, Mr. Silva. I need help. My heater isn’t working." The plumber asked, "Can you do this afternoon?" At exactly three o’clock, Mr. Silva arrived. He looked at the heater and said, "This is easy." After thirty minutes, the heater was working again! "Thank you, Mr. Silva. Now I can take a hot shower again!"

English

When we walk through the streets of an old city, it's impossible not to feel a certain magic in the air. The centuries-old buildings, with their facades worn by time, tell stories that books often cannot convey. However, despite many recognizing the value of historical and architectural heritage, the truth is that it faces constant threats. It is urgent that we reflect on what we lose when we let these witnesses of the past disappear. A city without memory is like a book without pages: it exists, but it says nothing. Monuments and historical buildings are not just stones or bricks; they are symbols of identity, struggles, and achievements that shaped the society we live in today. Therefore, preserving them is not a luxury but a necessity, so that future generations can understand where they came from. Nevertheless, many argue that rehabilitating these spaces is costly and time-consuming. While this is true, we cannot forget that the cost of loss is even greater, since it is something irretrievable. Moreover, historical heritage plays a crucial role in tourism and the local economy. Cities like Lisbon or Porto attract millions of visitors every year precisely because they offer a unique experience, where the old and the modern intertwine harmoniously. Thus, investing in the conservation of these spaces is not only a cultural issue but also an economic one. As long as there is political will and community involvement, it is possible to find solutions that benefit everyone. In short, protecting our heritage is protecting our history. It is not enough to admire these buildings from afar; it is essential that we take concrete steps to ensure they remain standing. After all, as the saying goes, "We do not inherit the Earth from our ancestors; we borrow it from our children."
