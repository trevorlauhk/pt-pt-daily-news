Yesterday, I went to the café with my friend Rita. We found a table near the window and ordered two coffees and a chocolate cake. While we waited, Rita told me about her vacation. She went to Lisbon last month and visited many beautiful places. "I rode the tram and took lots of photos," she said. I also talked about my week. "I worked a lot, but this weekend I’m going to rest," I explained. Rita laughed and said, "So, shall we go to the cinema on Saturday? There’s a new movie out!". After that, the waiter brought the coffee and the cake. The cake was delicious, but the coffee was a bit strong. Rita added more sugar and stirred it with a spoon. Before leaving, we paid the bill and agreed to meet again next week. "I loved this coffee time with you," Rita said. I agreed and replied, "Me too! See you on Saturday!".

English

In recent years, climate change has taken center stage in global conversations, and for good reason. Despite many governments and companies promising to reduce carbon emissions, the truth is that results are slow to appear. It is urgent that each of us reflects on the impact of our daily actions, since, even if it doesn’t seem like it, small changes can make a big difference. However, not all is lost. There are inspiring initiatives worldwide proving that sustainability is not just a distant dream. For example, cities like Copenhagen have already managed to significantly reduce their ecological footprint by implementing renewable energy and promoting efficient public transport. Nevertheless, for these solutions to become the norm, a collective mindset shift is necessary. Therefore, the question arises: are we willing to sacrifice some immediate comfort so that future generations may inherit a habitable planet? The answer depends on each of us, but one thing is certain: time to act is running out. As long as we start today, there is still hope. After all, as the saying goes, "prevention is better than cure."
