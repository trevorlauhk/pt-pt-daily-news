English

Yesterday, Maria went to the post office to send a package to her grandmother. Her grandmother lives in Porto and loves sweets. So, Maria decided to send a chocolate cake and some homemade jams. Before leaving home, she wrapped everything carefully. She put the cake in a small box and the jams in glass jars. Then, she wrote her grandmother’s address on a piece of paper and stuck it on the box. At the post office, the clerk weighed the package and said, "It will cost five euros." Maria paid and received a receipt. The clerk also gave her a tracking number. "Now, Grandma will be so happy!" Maria thought. She will call her grandmother tonight to tell her about the surprise. Later, she will check online to see if the package has arrived. Maria really enjoys sending things to her family. Before, she used to send letters, but now she prefers packages. It’s more fun!

English

In recent years, the internet has become a battleground where freedom of expression and misinformation clash daily. On one hand, social media allows anyone to share their opinions so that marginalized voices can finally be heard. However, this same freedom has a dark side: the spread of fake news, which travels faster than a virus. Despite many arguing that censorship is a threat to democracy, it is undeniable that something needs to change, since misinformation has already influenced elections, worsened health crises, and even incited violence. The great dilemma lies in knowing how to regulate this space without slipping into authoritarianism. Some argue that platforms should be more transparent about their algorithms so that users understand why they see certain content. Others suggest that a kind of «digital tribunal», made up of experts, should be implemented to assess questionable cases. However, who guarantees that these decisions will be impartial? And, more importantly, how can we prevent opportunistic governments from using these rules to silence legitimate criticism? What is at stake is not just the truth, but also our ability to trust one another. Therefore, instead of waiting for solutions to come only from above, perhaps it is time for each of us to take responsibility. Questioning sources, verifying facts, and thinking twice before sharing are small gestures that, as long as they are practiced by many, can make a difference. After all, freedom of expression should not be a shield for lies, but rather a tool to build a more informed and just society.
