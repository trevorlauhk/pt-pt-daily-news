Yesterday, Ana didn’t feel well. She had a headache and a fever since the morning. So, she decided to go to the pharmacy near her house. The pharmacy on Rua Nova is small, but it always has everything people need. When she arrived, Ana saw Dona Clara, the pharmacist. "Good morning, Ana! What happened?" she asked with a smile. "Good morning, Dona Clara. I have a headache and a fever. I need some medicine, please." Dona Clara listened carefully and then said, "I’ll see what we have." After a few minutes, Dona Clara returned with a box of tablets. "This medicine is good for fever and pain. Take one tablet every eight hours, okay?" Ana paid and thanked her. "Get well, Ana! If you don’t feel better tomorrow, go to the doctor," said Dona Clara. Ana left the pharmacy and went home. She took the medicine and rested. Today, she already feels much better!

English

In the 21st century, education has ceased to be merely a fundamental right and has become the key that can open—or close—doors in an increasingly complex and interconnected world. Despite technological advances having democratized access to knowledge, many experts question whether schools are truly prepared to train critical and adaptable citizens. It is essential that education systems reinvent themselves so that students not only absorb information but also develop skills such as creativity and problem-solving. However, not everything is rosy. Since social inequality persists, many young people continue to be left behind without access to basic resources. Consequently, education, which should be a social elevator, ends up perpetuating disparities. Nevertheless, some believe the solution lies in investing in innovative methodologies, such as project-based learning, provided there is political will and adequate funding. It is urgent that governments and civil society recognize that education is not an expense but an investment. After all, as Nelson Mandela said, "Education is the most powerful weapon to change the world." Therefore, it is up to all of us to ensure that this weapon is accessible to everyone, regardless of their background or economic status.
