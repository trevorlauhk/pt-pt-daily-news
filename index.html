English

Yesterday was Sofia’s birthday. She decided to go to an Italian restaurant with her boyfriend, Pedro. When they arrived, the waiter said, "Good evening! A table for two?" They sat near the window. Pedro picked up the menu and read aloud: "Today we have spaghetti bolognese, lasagna, and four-cheese pizza." Sofia smiled and said, "I want pizza, please. And you?" Pedro thought for a moment and answered, "I’ll have spaghetti. I also want a salad because I’m very hungry." Then, the waiter asked, "And to drink?" Sofia ordered an orange juice, and Pedro chose water. While they waited, Sofia looked at Pedro and said, "This restaurant is very nice. I like the decoration." When the food arrived, they were both happy. They ate, talked, and laughed a lot. At the end, Pedro asked for the bill and paid with his card. Before leaving, Sofia said, "Thank you for dinner, Pedro! It was perfect."

English

In a world increasingly dominated by technology and haste, it's easy to forget the value of art and culture. However, these are not mere luxuries or pastimes; they are, in fact, fundamental pillars that sustain a society's identity and cohesion. While many argue that resources should be directed toward "more practical" areas like the economy or health, the truth is that art has the power to transform lives in ways that are not always measurable. Imagine a city without museums, without theaters, without music in the streets. It would be a cold, soulless place where people, though they might live, would have no opportunity to dream. Art, whether visual, musical, or literary, allows us to express emotions that would otherwise remain trapped inside us. Moreover, it is through culture that we preserve our traditions and pass on values to future generations so that they do not lose their connection to the past. Yet, art is not just a mirror of what we were; it is also a window into what we might become. When a young person discovers theater or painting, they may find a purpose they had never before considered. Therefore, investing in culture is investing in human potential, since it stimulates creativity, empathy, and critical thinking—skills essential in an ever-changing world. So, instead of questioning whether art is necessary, we should ask ourselves what our lives would be like without it. After all, a society that values culture is one that breathes, feels, and, above all, understands itself and others.
