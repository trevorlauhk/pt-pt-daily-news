English

Yesterday, Pedro went to the mobile phone store because his old plan wasn’t good anymore. He was paying a lot of money every month, but he only used 2GB of internet. The salesman, Mr. Costa, smiled and said, "We have great options for you!" First, Pedro saw a plan with 10GB for 15 euros. He thought, "It’s cheap, but do I really need that much internet?" Then, Mr. Costa showed another plan with 5GB for 10 euros. This plan also had unlimited calls to all networks. Pedro liked this one better, but he still had doubts. So, Mr. Costa explained, "This plan is perfect for you." Last month, you only used 2GB, right? Pedro agreed and said, "I’ll take this one!" Before leaving, Pedro asked, "Can I change my plan later if I need to?" Mr. Costa replied, "Of course! You can change it anytime you want, no problem." Pedro left the store very happy. Now, he’ll pay less and have more internet.

English

Almost fifty years ago, Portugal woke up to a new dawn. April 25, 1974, was not just another day in the country's history; it was the moment when the Portuguese decided it was time to end decades of dictatorship. Although many young people today did not live through that era, it is essential that they understand the impact this event had—and continues to have—on today's society. In the early hours of that day, the military from the Armed Forces Movement (MFA) took to the streets, not to wage war, but to return freedom to the people. However, what could have been a simple coup turned into a peaceful revolution, as the population joined en masse, placing carnations in the barrels of rifles. It is impressive to think that, in just a few hours, a regime that had lasted since 1933 fell without bloodshed. Therefore, April 25 is not just a commemorative date; it is a symbol of hope and unity. Yet, not everything was easy after the revolution. The transition to democracy required sacrifices, and many Portuguese had to rebuild their lives in a changing country. Still, it is undeniable that, since democracy was established, Portugal has taken giant steps in areas such as education, human rights, and European integration. So that future generations do not forget this legacy, it is crucial that schools continue to teach the history of April 25 with the seriousness it deserves. Today, when we look back, we realize that this revolution was not just a historical milestone, but also a reminder that freedom is not a given. It is our duty to preserve it, so that the mistakes of the past are never repeated.
