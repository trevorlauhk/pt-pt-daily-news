Yesterday, Pedro went to fill up his car. He stopped at the gas station near his house. Before starting, he checked the oil and water because his dad always says, "Better safe than sorry." Then, he opened the fuel tank cap and chose the fuel. Pedro wasn’t sure which was best, so he asked the attendant: "Regular or premium gasoline?" The attendant smiled and replied, "Premium is better for the engine, but it costs a bit more." Pedro thought about it and decided on premium. While the car was filling up, he went to pay. At the store, he also bought a coffee and a pastry. When he returned, the car was ready. He thanked the attendant and left. On the way to work, the car ran very smoothly. Pedro was happy because he made a good choice!

English

In recent years, artificial intelligence (AI) has been transforming the world in ways that, just a few decades ago, would have seemed like science fiction. From virtual assistants that answer our questions to algorithms that diagnose diseases with precision, AI is everywhere. However, despite all the benefits it brings, some fear that this technology may one day slip out of our control. Should we see it as an ally or a threat? One of AI’s greatest advantages is its ability to process vast amounts of data in seconds, which allows, for example, the optimization of industrial processes or the improvement of energy efficiency. Moreover, in fields like medicine, AI already saves lives, as it can detect patterns that humans might never notice. Nevertheless, it is important that we do not let enthusiasm blind us. There are real risks, such as job losses to machines or the possibility of AI being used with malicious intent, like in the creation of fake news or cyberattacks. Therefore, the question is not whether we should use AI, but how to do so responsibly. It is crucial that governments and companies establish clear rules so that this technology is developed for the benefit of all. Despite some fears being understandable, we cannot ignore AI’s potential to solve global problems, such as climate change or hunger. Thus, it is up to us to ensure its use is ethical and safe, so that future generations may inherit a better world.
