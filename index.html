Liberdade de expressão na era digital: até onde vai o direito de dizer o que se pensa?

English

Freedom of expression is one of the fundamental pillars of any democracy, since it allows citizens to express their opinions without fear of state censorship. However, in the digital age, this fundamental right faces an unprecedented challenge: the massive proliferation of online misinformation spreading at dizzying speed. Many argue that, provided no obvious crimes are committed, people should be able to publish whatever they want on social networks. However, this perspective ignores that lies, when they spread rapidly across digital platforms, can cause real and irreparable damage to society, as we saw during the pandemic or in recent electoral processes. Therefore, it becomes imperative to find a delicate balance between protecting free expression and the urgent need to ensure that the information conveyed is at least true. This constant tension between the right to speak freely and the duty not to lie places us before a particularly complex ethical dilemma in our hyperconnected society. Some defend that digital platforms should regulate clearly false content, in order that users are not deceived by deliberately fabricated news. Others, on the other hand, fear that such control could lead to arbitrary censorship, despite recognizing that misinformation is deeply harmful. It is essential that each of us develops critical thinking so that we can distinguish verifiable facts from mere rumors, even when the news pleases us or confirms our most deeply rooted beliefs. The solution does not involve eliminating freedom of expression, but rather promoting media literacy on a large scale. When we educate people to question sources and verify facts before sharing, we are strengthening the foundations of democracy. Thus, although it is difficult to define clear and consensual limits, it is essential that society debates these issues openly, so that we may find together a path that protects both truth and individual freedom.
