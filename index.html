English

Yesterday, Pedro went to the bank to open an account. He was a little nervous because it was his first time. Before leaving home, he checked his documents: his citizen card, tax number, and proof of address. When he arrived at the bank, there were many people in line. He waited fifteen minutes and then spoke to the clerk. — Good morning! I’d like to open an account, please — Pedro said. — Of course! Do you have all the documents? — the lady asked. — Yes, I do. Here they are — he replied, handing over the papers. The clerk looked at the documents and smiled. — Everything is fine. Now, you just need to sign here and choose a secret code. Pedro signed and chose an easy-to-remember code. Then, the lady gave him a temporary card and explained: — In a week, you’ll receive your permanent card at home. Pedro thanked her and left the bank very happy. Now, he can pay bills online and save money. It was easier than he thought!

O Futuro da Mobilidade: Será que os Transportes Públicos Podem Salvar as Nossas Cidades?

English

Modern cities face a growing dilemma: how to ensure that millions of people can move efficiently without turning traffic into a daily nightmare? Although many still prefer the comfort of their private cars, public transport emerges as the most sustainable—and therefore most urgent—solution. However, for this change to happen, it is essential that governments invest not only in infrastructure but also in policies that encourage the use of the metro, bus, and train. One of the biggest advantages of public transport is the reduction of pollution. If everyone chose to leave their car at home, air quality would improve significantly, which, in turn, would have a positive impact on public health. However, not everything is perfect: many citizens complain about overcrowding and frequent delays. It is therefore necessary that authorities take measures so that the service becomes more reliable and comfortable. Furthermore, urban mobility is not just about moving people from point A to point B. It is also about rethinking public space, creating bike lanes and pedestrian zones that allow for a more harmonious coexistence between pedestrians, cyclists, and vehicles. Since cities continue to grow, it is imperative that smarter and more inclusive models be adopted. In conclusion, although the path to ideal urban mobility is still long, public transport represents a crucial step. As long as there is political will and citizen participation, it is possible to build a future where cities are cleaner, safer, and more accessible for everyone.
