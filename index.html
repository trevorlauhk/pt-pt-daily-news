Mrs. Ana has a very old red car. She likes it a lot, but the car is already many years old and sometimes makes strange noises. Yesterday, the car wouldn’t start. She tried several times but couldn’t get it to work. So, Mrs. Ana got worried and decided to take the car to the garage. This morning, she called the mechanic and scheduled an appointment for three in the afternoon. Before leaving home, she checked the oil and water but didn’t see anything wrong. Then, she called a taxi to go to the garage. When she arrived, the mechanic, Mr. João, was already waiting. "Let’s see what’s going on," he said with a smile. Mr. João examined the car for half an hour and then said, "It’s nothing serious!" Mrs. Ana felt relieved. She paid the bill and thanked Mr. João. Now, Mrs. Ana is going home happy.

In recent years, Artificial Intelligence (AI) has gained an increasingly significant place in our lives, transforming how we work, learn, and even relate to one another. Although many fear that machines may replace humans in various fields, it is important to recognize that AI, when used ethically and responsibly, can be a powerful ally. However, we cannot ignore the challenges this technology presents, since its impact on society is not yet fully understood. On one hand, AI offers countless advantages. From automating repetitive tasks to personalizing services like movie recommendations or medical diagnoses, the possibilities are nearly endless. Consequently, many argue that AI can free humans from monotonous work, allowing us to focus on more creative and meaningful activities. Nevertheless, some contend that, if clear limits are not established, this technology could deepen social inequalities, since not everyone will have access to the same opportunities. Furthermore, the issue of privacy arises. With the growing amount of data we share online, it is crucial that strict regulations exist so that our personal information is not used abusively. Although some governments are already taking steps to protect citizens, there is still a long way to go. Therefore, it is essential that each of us reflects on the role we want AI to play in the future, for only then can we ensure it is used for the common good. In short, AI is neither inherently good nor bad—it all depends on how we use it. If we can balance innovation and ethics, we may create a world where technology and humanity coexist in harmony. After all, the future is not written, and it is up to us to decide what story we want to tell.
