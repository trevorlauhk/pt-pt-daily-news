English

Yesterday was a special day for João. He got his driver’s license two weeks ago and now has his first car! It’s a small, red, and very economical car. But this morning, João saw that the tank was almost empty. "I need to fill up," he thought. João had never filled up a car by himself. He remembered his father’s instructions. João arrived at the gas station and did everything as his father said. But when it was time to pay, he got nervous. The attendant asked: "Card or cash?" João didn’t know what to say. Then, he took his card out of his wallet and replied: "By card, please." After paying, João smiled. "I already know how to fill up!" And so, João went back home happy, with a full tank and a heart full of pride.

Turismo em Portugal: Bênção ou Maldição para as Cidades Históricas?

English

In recent years, Portugal has become one of the most sought-after tourist destinations in Europe. Lisbon, Porto, and the Algarve attract millions of visitors annually, which, at first glance, seems like excellent news for the economy. However, is this unchecked growth not turning into a threat to our historic cities? Although tourism generates jobs and wealth, many residents complain that urban centers are losing their identity. Housing prices have skyrocketed, forcing families to move to peripheral areas since they can no longer afford rents in the city center. Furthermore, traditional shops are being replaced by establishments aimed at tourists, such as fast-food restaurants or souvenir shops. Consequently, it is urgent that the government implement measures to balance economic benefits with the preservation of heritage. Nevertheless, not everything is negative. Tourism has also brought investment in infrastructure and promoted the internationalization of Portuguese culture. In order for this sector to continue being a positive force, sustainable planning is necessary. For instance, cities like Barcelona or Venice have already adopted limits on the number of visitors, a solution that Portugal could consider. After all, the goal should not be to ban tourism but to ensure that it enriches, rather than destroys, what makes us unique. Therefore, it is up to all of us to reflect: to what extent are we willing to sacrifice our quality of life in the name of progress? Perhaps the answer lies in finding a balance before it is too late.
