Yesterday, Ana and João went to the beach with their friends. They left home early because they wanted to make the most of the day. The sun was strong, but the wind was also blowing a little. When they arrived, Ana took off her shoes and ran into the water. "It's cold!" she shouted, but then she laughed and swam a bit. João preferred to stay on the sand. He brought a book and a big towel. He lay down and read for an hour. Later, the friends played volleyball and had a picnic. They ate sandwiches, fruit, and drank juice. In the afternoon, the sky became cloudy. "Let's go before it rains," said João. They packed everything up and returned home. "It was a perfect day! Shall we do it again next week?" "Of course! But next time, I’ll bring sunscreen."

English

We live in an era where cities are growing at a rapid pace, and with them, the challenges of urban mobility. Although many still prefer private cars, it is undeniable that public transport plays a crucial role in reducing traffic and pollution. However, for this solution to be truly effective, authorities must invest not only in infrastructure but also in policies that encourage its use. One of the greatest advantages of public transport is its ability to connect people, regardless of their economic status. Nevertheless, for everyone to benefit from this network, it is essential that there be comprehensive and frequent coverage. For example, in Lisbon, despite the existence of several metro and bus lines, there are still peripheral areas where service is scarce. Therefore, it is urgent to expand these networks so that no one is left behind. Moreover, technology can be a great ally. Nowadays, there are apps that allow real-time trip planning, which greatly facilitates users' lives. However, not everyone has access to smartphones or the internet, so it is important that these solutions do not exclude the most vulnerable. Since mobility is a right for all, cities must ensure that options are inclusive. Consequently, the future of our cities depends largely on the choices we make today. If we continue to prioritize individual transport, we risk living in congested and polluted metropolises. But if we opt for efficient and sustainable public systems, we can build a healthier and more equitable environment for future generations. After all, a city in motion is a city with life — and it is up to us to decide what kind of life we want.
