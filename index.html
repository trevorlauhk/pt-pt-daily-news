In the 21st century, education faces a dilemma that, although not new, has become more urgent: should it prepare students only for the job market or, consequently, shape critical and conscious citizens? Many argue that, since technology advances at a breakneck pace, schools should focus on technical skills. However, is that enough? After all, what good is a highly qualified professional if they cannot reflect on the impact of their actions on society? The truth is that education cannot be reduced to mere skills training. It is crucial that students learn to think independently, question the status quo, and collaborate with others, so that they can tackle global challenges like climate change or social inequalities. Yet, many education systems still resist this shift, favoring memorization over creativity. Despite some arguing that schools already have too many responsibilities, we cannot ignore that education is the foundation of a fairer society. Therefore, it is essential that governments invest in innovative pedagogical models, provided that they promote not only academic success but also personal development. Only then will we be able to shape individuals capable of building a better future for all.
