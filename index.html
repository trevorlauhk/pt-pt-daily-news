English

João loves reading, but he doesn’t have many books at home. One day, his teacher says at school: "You can borrow books from the city library. It’s easy and free!" João is very happy and decides to go on Saturday. On Saturday morning, João goes to the library. He feels a little nervous because he doesn’t know how it works. So, he asks a kind lady who works there: "Excuse me, how can I borrow a book?" The lady smiles and answers: "First, you need to register. Then, you can choose the books you want." João registers and starts looking. He likes adventures, so he picks three books: one about pirates, another about explorers, and a mystery book. Then, he takes the books to the counter. The lady stamps the books and says: "You can come back for more when you finish reading these!" João leaves the library very happy. He already knows he’ll come back many times. Now, he has lots of books to read and doesn’t need to spend money.

O Futuro da Mobilidade: Será que os Transportes Públicos Podem Salvar as Nossas Cidades?

B1-B2 (DEPLE/DIPLE)

English

In recent years, cities have faced a growing problem: too many cars on the streets. Although many citizens still prefer the comfort of their own vehicles, the truth is that congested traffic not only increases pollution but also steals precious hours from our day. Therefore, it is urgent to rethink how we get around. Public transport, however, emerges as a viable solution, since, if well-planned, it can significantly reduce the number of private vehicles. Nevertheless, for this to happen, authorities must invest in modern and accessible infrastructure. Imagine a city where buses and subways run so frequently that no one has to wait more than five minutes. Furthermore, if there were safe and well-marked bike lanes, many would choose bicycles, so that the environment would also benefit. Of course, not everyone lives near a station, but as long as there is an integrated transport network, even the most peripheral areas could be served efficiently. Although some argue that public transport is slow or unreliable, technology already allows for improvements in these aspects. Real-time apps, therefore, help users plan their trips accurately. What is often lacking is the political will to implement these changes. If we all made an effort to leave the car at home at least a few days a week, cities would become cleaner, quieter, and, above all, more humane.
