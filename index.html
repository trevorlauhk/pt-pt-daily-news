When she arrived, she saw a short line. The lady in front of her bought cough syrup. Then, it was Ana’s turn. She said to the pharmacist, "Good morning! I need something for my headache and to lower my fever." The pharmacist smiled and asked, "Are you allergic to any medication?" Ana replied, "No, I’m not." She hopes to feel better tomorrow!

English

In recent years, social media has become the main stage for public debate. Millions of people share opinions, news, and even emotions in an instant, creating an unprecedented global web of communication. However, this brave new world comes with an increasingly worrying problem: disinformation. How, then, can we balance freedom of expression—a fundamental right in any democracy—with the need to combat lies that can endanger public health, political stability, or even human lives? While many argue that the internet should remain a free space where everyone can express themselves without filters, it is undeniable that the spread of falsehoods has become a real threat. Just recall cases of conspiracy theories that led to violent acts or fake news about vaccines, which caused some people to refuse essential treatments. Yet, banning certain discourses, even if false, raises ethical and practical questions. Who decides what is true? And how can we ensure these decisions are not used to silence uncomfortable, though legitimate, voices? One possible solution is to educate users so they can identify reliable sources and question what they read. Additionally, platforms could implement more robust verification systems, provided they do not compromise the diversity of opinions. After all, the goal is not to censor but to ensure that online dialogue is constructive and fact-based. If we want freedom of expression to remain a pillar of society, we must act responsibly—because, as the saying goes, «with great power comes great responsibility».
