Yesterday, Ana decided to send a package to her grandmother. Her grandmother lives in Porto, and Ana lives in Lisbon. She made a chocolate cake and wanted to share it with her. In the morning, Ana went to the supermarket and bought a large box. Then, she put the cake inside and wrapped everything with gift paper. She also wrote a card: "To the best grandmother in the world!" In the afternoon, Ana went to the post office. There, a friendly clerk helped her. First, she weighed the package. Then, Ana paid eight euros. The clerk said, "The package will arrive tomorrow morning." Ana was happy. Before leaving, she bought a pretty stamp for the card. Now, she will wait for her grandmother’s reply. Maybe her grandmother will call tonight! Ana also wants to send a package to her brother, but it will be more expensive.

A Balança Digital: Até Onde Vai a Liberdade de Expressão na Era da Desinformação?

English

In recent years, the internet has become the main stage for public debate. However, this communication revolution has brought with it a complex dilemma: how to balance freedom of expression with the fight against disinformation? Although many argue that any restriction is a threat to fundamental rights, it is undeniable that the spread of fake news has serious consequences, from social polarization to election manipulation. Since social media has become the primary source of information for millions of people, it is crucial that mechanisms exist to ensure the accuracy of shared content. Yet, who decides what is true or false? Censorship, even if well-intentioned, can easily become a tool of oppression, especially in authoritarian regimes. Therefore, the solution is not to ban, but to educate. In order for citizens to develop critical thinking, investment in media literacy from school is necessary. It is true that freedom of expression is a pillar of democracy, but this does not mean it should be absolute. There are clear limits, such as hate speech or incitement to violence, which are already regulated by law. In short, the challenge is not simple, but ignoring it would be even more dangerous. After all, in an era where anyone can publish a lie with global reach, protecting the truth is also protecting freedom.
