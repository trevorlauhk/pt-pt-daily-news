English

Yesterday morning I went to the post office because I wanted to send a package to my sister in Coimbra. She has her birthday tomorrow, so I took a special gift. I took a book of Portuguese poetry and a very beautiful red scarf. I found the scarf in an old shop in the city centre. I arrived at the post office at ten in the morning. There were many people in the queue, but I waited patiently because I was not in a hurry. My turn arrived and I said to the employee: "Good morning, I want to send this to Coimbra, please." "It is a normal package." He asked if I wanted insurance and I said it was not necessary. Then he weighed the small box and said it cost five euros. I paid by debit card and received a receipt with the tracking number. The employee said the package arrived in Coimbra in two or three working days. I was very happy because my sister is going to receive the gift in time for the birthday party. Before leaving the post office, I also bought ten stamps to send postcards to my friends in Lisbon. Tomorrow morning I am going to call my sister to tell her that I already sent the package.

English

It is undeniable that Portuguese cities are true open-air museums, where every corner tells a different story. However, do we give our historical and architectural heritage the value it deserves? I think not, since we prioritize economic progress at the expense of our collective memory. Although there are strict heritage protection laws, many buildings remain at risk of disappearing. It is essential that authorities invest in rehabilitating these spaces, so that future generations can understand where they come from. Provided there is genuine political will, it is possible to reconcile urban development with preserving the past. However, these spaces must be alive, with cultural activities taking place in them. In this way, heritage becomes an integral part of daily life. Consequently, when we walk through the streets of Lisbon or Porto, we witness centuries of history. Despite some recent progress, there is still a long way to go. Therefore, it is up to each of us to demand that what makes us unique in the world be valued. Future generations would thank us greatly if we acted responsibly now. Only thus will we ensure that our heritage is not just a testimony of the past, but a bridge to the future.
