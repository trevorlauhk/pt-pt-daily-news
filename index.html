English

Yesterday, Pedro went to the City Hall for the first time. He needed an important document for work. Before leaving home, he checked his backpack: he had his ID card, the filled-out form, and a pen. "Better safe than sorry," he thought. When he arrived, he saw a long line. "This is going to take a while," he sighed. But after ten minutes, the line moved quickly. A kind lady helped him right away. Pedro explained that he needed a residence certificate. While he waited, Pedro looked around. Five minutes later, the lady handed him the document. Pedro thanked her and left happily. On his way home, he decided to stop by a café. "Today, I deserve a cake and a coffee," he said to himself.

Contratos de Telecomunicações: Quando Reclamar Não é (Só) um Direito, mas um Dever

B1-B2 (DEPLE/DIPLE)
🐶 Oreo Sir 小 Tips

English

Who hasn’t felt trapped in a telecommunications contract that, despite being advertised as "the best on the market," ends up becoming a real headache? It’s common to sign an agreement expecting everything to go as planned, yet when problems arise—slow speeds, unjustified charges, or non-existent customer service—many of us hesitate to complain. However, it’s essential to know that, since we pay for a service, we have the right to demand quality. Although bureaucracy and the fear of retaliation may discourage us, it’s important to remember that companies depend on customers to survive. Therefore, if we don’t speak up, they will continue to offer mediocre services without consequences. In order for the situation to improve, we must document all failures, contact the company in writing, and, if necessary, turn to regulatory bodies like ANACOM. Of course, it’s not always easy to stay calm when we feel deceived, but it’s crucial that we act firmly and politely. After all, complaining is not just a right, but a civic duty that benefits not only us but also other consumers who may be in the same situation. So, the next time you feel wronged, don’t stay silent—demand what is rightfully yours!
