English

In recent years, the idea of a four-day workweek has gained traction in several countries, and Portugal is no exception. However, although some studies suggest this change could boost productivity and workers' well-being, there are those who argue it would bring more challenges than benefits. But are we ready to rethink the traditional concept of work so that quality of life can improve significantly? One of the main advantages highlighted by supporters of this proposal is the possibility for employees to have more time to rest and dedicate themselves to personal activities. Nevertheless, it is important to consider that not all sectors can easily adapt to this model. For instance, areas like healthcare or hospitality, which require constant presence, could face logistical difficulties. Therefore, a widespread implementation might not be feasible without prior adjustments. Moreover, some fear that, even if the weekly working hours remain the same, the accumulated fatigue from longer workdays could cancel out the expected benefits. Since the balance between professional and personal life is fundamental, it would be necessary to ensure that workers do not feel overwhelmed. As long as there is flexibility and dialogue between employers and employees, this transition could be successful. In conclusion, the four-day week is not a magical solution, but it could be an important step in the right direction. What is certain is that if we want to build a healthier and happier society, we will have to rethink how we work. After all, the goal should not just be to produce more, but to live better.
