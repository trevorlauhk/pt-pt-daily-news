English

In recent years, social media has become a constant presence in the lives of young people. While it offers unique opportunities for connection and learning, many experts question whether these digital spaces are actually shaping behaviors in worrying ways. It’s undeniable that platforms like Instagram or TikTok allow teenagers to share their passions and discover new cultures, however, the price to pay may be high. One of the biggest concerns is how social media distorts the perception of reality. Many young people, since they spend hours consuming idealized content, end up developing unrealistic expectations about life, body image, or success. Consequently, self-esteem can be seriously affected, especially when they compare themselves to influencers who, despite seeming perfect, often hide their own insecurities. Furthermore, the fear of missing out (FOMO) leads many to feel anxious if they are not constantly online, as if real life were not enough. However, not everything is negative. Social media can also be a powerful tool for activism and education. Movements like #FridaysForFuture have shown that young people are capable of organizing and fighting for important causes, as long as they have access to information and platforms that amplify their voices. Therefore, the challenge is not to eliminate social media, but to learn to use it critically and balanced, so that it does not become an invisible prison, but rather a mirror that reflects the best in each of us.
