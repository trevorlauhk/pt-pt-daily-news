English

When we think of Portuguese gastronomy, it’s impossible not to associate it with comforting dishes like *bacalhau à brás* or a simple *pão com chouriço*. However, in recent years, something extraordinary has been happening in the country’s kitchens: a true culinary revolution that, while embracing global influences, never loses sight of the roots that make it unique. So, what has changed for «new Portuguese gastronomy» to be talked about so much today? Firstly, it’s important to recognize that tradition has never been abandoned. On the contrary, many chefs have sought to recreate ancestral recipes so that new generations can rediscover forgotten flavors. Yet, what is most surprising is how local ingredients, such as olive oil from Trás-os-Montes or mushrooms from the Alentejo, are now combined with modern techniques. As a result, dishes that were once seen as «grandma’s food» have gained new life, attracting both tourists and young Portuguese people. Of course, this evolution didn’t happen by chance. Since Portugal has become an increasingly popular tourist destination, the pressure to innovate has grown. Although some purists fear that these changes might strip Portuguese cuisine of its identity, the truth is that the result has been a harmonious fusion between the old and the contemporary. Finally, it’s worth reflecting on the impact of this transformation. While Portuguese gastronomy has become more sophisticated, it has also remained accessible. Therefore, whether in a neighborhood *tasco* or a Michelin-starred restaurant, there’s no shortage of opportunities to celebrate the richness of a cuisine that, though it evolves, never forgets where it came from.
