English

Last night, João was watching TV on the sofa. Suddenly, he heard a strange noise in the kitchen. At first, he thought it was the cat, but the noise continued. João slowly got up and went to see what it was. When he opened the kitchen door, he saw an unknown man messing with the window! João was very scared and didn’t know what to do. Then, he remembered: "I have to call the police!". He ran to the bedroom, closed the door, and grabbed his phone. He dialed 112 and waited. A calm voice answered: "Emergency, how can I help you?". João quickly explained everything and gave his address. The police arrived in five minutes and arrested the man. Afterwards, an officer told João: "You did the right thing by calling. You’re safe now." João sighed in relief and thanked them. He’ll never forget this night!

English

In recent years, Portugal has stood out as one of Europe’s most committed countries to the energy transition. Despite facing economic and logistical challenges, the potential of renewable energy in our territory is undeniable. The sun shines almost year-round in the Algarve, strong winds blow along the west coast, and rivers, though less predictable, remain a valuable source of hydroelectric power. However, for this green future to become a tangible reality, it is necessary that the government and companies invest not only in infrastructure but also in innovation. One of Portugal’s greatest advantages is its geographical location. Since we have access to abundant natural resources, it would be a waste not to make the most of them. Nevertheless, dependence on favorable weather conditions can be an obstacle. Therefore, it is crucial to develop energy storage solutions so that we can ensure a stable supply, even when the sun isn’t shining or the wind isn’t blowing. Furthermore, public acceptance is key. Many citizens still fear that renewable energy could drive up electricity costs or harm the landscape. Although these concerns are understandable, recent studies show that, in the long run, the economic and environmental benefits far outweigh the initial costs. Thus, it is essential that there be open dialogue between authorities and the public so that everyone understands the advantages of this shift. Portugal’s energy future can be bright, as long as we continue to bet on sustainable policies and clean technologies. If we work together, we can not only reduce our ecological footprint but also become an example for the rest of Europe. After all, who wouldn’t want to live in a country where progress goes hand in hand with nature?
