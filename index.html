English

Yesterday, Pedro's car made a strange noise. He stopped on the side of the road and called the garage. "My car is sick," he said. The mechanic replied, "Don’t worry, you can bring it here tomorrow." This morning, Pedro woke up early. He had breakfast and then called a taxi. The taxi took him to the garage. The mechanic was already waiting. "Good morning! What happened to your car?" he asked. Pedro explained, "Yesterday, it made a very loud noise and then stopped." The mechanic looked at the car and said, "I’ll see what’s wrong." Two hours later, Pedro returned to the garage. "Everything is fine now! It was just a small problem." Pedro was very happy and took the car home.

Portugal à Beira de um Sonho Verde: Será a Energia Renovável o Nosso Futuro?

English

For decades, Portugal has dreamed of a future where renewable energy is not just an alternative, but the main source of electricity. Although the country has already taken significant steps in this direction, it remains to be seen whether we can truly achieve the energy independence we so desire. One thing is certain: the potential exists, however, the challenges are not few. In 2023, over 60% of the electricity consumed in Portugal came from renewable sources, a historic milestone that shows when there is political will and investment, results appear. However, for this growth to continue, it is essential to invest not only in technology but also in modernizing the electrical grids. After all, what use is producing clean energy if we cannot distribute it efficiently? Furthermore, it is crucial that the population is aware of the importance of reducing consumption, since sustainability does not depend only on governments, but on each of us. Despite the progress, some argue that Portugal is still far from being self-sufficient. For example, in months with less wind or rain, the production of wind and hydroelectric energy decreases, forcing the country to resort to non-renewable sources. Therefore, it is necessary to diversify energy sources, including solar, which has enormous potential in our territory. In order for this goal to be achieved, the government must create tax incentives for families and companies that want to install solar panels. In conclusion, Portugal has everything it takes to become an example in the energy transition, provided there is a serious commitment from all sectors of society. If we continue on this path, perhaps one day we can say that we live in a country where clean energy is not just a dream, but a consolidated reality.
