English

Maria is 22 years old and wants to rent her first apartment. She lives with her parents, but now she’s going to start working and needs a place of her own. On Saturday, Maria goes to see a small apartment in the city center. The apartment is nice and has a living room, a bedroom, a kitchen, and a bathroom. The rent is 500 euros per month, but Maria only earns 700 euros. She gets a little worried, but the landlord says, "You can pay 450 euros if you help with the building’s cleaning." Maria really likes the apartment. She thinks, "It’s close to work and there’s a great café on the corner." So, she decides to rent it. On Sunday, Maria signs the contract and pays the first month’s rent. Then, she goes to the supermarket to buy a few things: a rug, a lamp, and a vase. She also takes a painting from her mother to decorate the living room. At night, Maria calls her best friend and says, "I’m moving next week!" Her friend replies, "Everything will be fine! And I’ll help you organize everything." The next day, Maria starts packing her bags. She knows it’s going to be a new adventure!

English

Choosing between living in the city or the countryside defines not just our daily lives, but also how we experience the world. While many believe that urban hustle is synonymous with success, others argue that the tranquility of the countryside offers an unmatched quality of life. But is there really a right answer to this question? In the city, everything seems to happen at a frantic pace. Job opportunities are more abundant, services are just a click away, and cultural life is vibrant. However, this frenzy comes at a cost: stress, pollution, and the loneliness that sometimes lurks behind the bright lights. It’s important that we recognize that, although the city offers convenience, it doesn’t always provide the peace we need. On the other hand, the countryside invites us to slow down. Contact with nature, clean air, and a tighter-knit community are aspects that many city dwellers envy. As long as basic services like healthcare and education are accessible, living in the countryside can be a deeply enriching experience. In order for us to truly appreciate this simplicity, we must be willing to give up certain conveniences. Therefore, the choice between city and countryside shouldn’t be seen as a competition, but rather as a reflection on what we value in life. Since everyone has different priorities, the most important thing is to find a balance that makes us happy. After all, the heart of life doesn’t beat strongest in a specific place, but where we feel at home.
