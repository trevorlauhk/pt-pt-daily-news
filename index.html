English

In recent years, Portugal has stood out as one of Europe’s most committed countries to the energy transition. Although we still partly depend on fossil fuels, the potential of renewable energy in our territory is undeniable. The sun, wind, and water are not only abundant resources but also offer a unique opportunity for the country to become self-sufficient and even an exporter of clean energy. However, for this scenario to materialize, continuous investment in infrastructure and technological innovation is essential. With over 300 sunny days a year in some regions, it’s only natural that solar energy is an obvious bet. Nevertheless, despite the existence of several solar and wind farms, there is still much to be done. For instance, it is necessary that the electrical grids be modernized so that the energy produced can be distributed efficiently. Furthermore, it is crucial that government policies encourage not only large companies but also ordinary citizens to adopt renewable solutions. While most Portuguese support clean energy, some fear that wind or solar farms may affect the landscape or even tourism. Therefore, it is important that there be open dialogue between authorities, companies, and local communities so that everyone understands the long-term benefits. After all, once we reduce our dependence on polluting sources, we not only protect the environment but also create jobs and stimulate the economy. The future of renewable energy in Portugal is promising, but it requires collective effort. If we continue on this path, it is possible that, within a few decades, the country will no longer be just an example in Europe but a global leader in sustainable energy production. And who knows? Perhaps one day, instead of importing oil, we will export sun and wind to the whole world.
