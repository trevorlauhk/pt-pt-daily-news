English

Rui loves reading, but he doesn’t have many books at home. One day, his teacher says in class: "You can borrow books from the school library. It’s easy and free!" Rui is very happy and decides to go to the library after class. When he arrives at the library, he sees many shelves full of books. He doesn’t know where to start. Then, he approaches the librarian, Dona Clara, and asks: "Good afternoon! How can I borrow a book?" Dona Clara smiles and replies: "First, choose a book you like." Rui walks through the shelves and finds a book about dinosaurs. He loves dinosaurs! Then, he goes to the counter and hands the book and his card to Dona Clara. She types something on the computer and says: "You can take the book home for two weeks." Rui thanks her and leaves the library very happy. That night, he reads the whole book and dreams about dinosaurs. The next day, he tells his friends: "The library is fantastic! I’m going to borrow more books there!"

Sabores que Contam Histórias: Como a Gastronomia Portuguesa se Reinventou

B1-B2 (DEPLE/DIPLE)

English

Portuguese gastronomy is an open book where every dish tells a page of our history. Although many associate Portugal only with codfish or pastéis de nata, the truth is that our cuisine has evolved surprisingly over the centuries. It’s fascinating to observe how simple ingredients like olive oil, garlic, or bread have transformed into symbols of national identity, even if, at first glance, they seem ordinary. However, this evolution did not happen by chance. During the Age of Discoveries, the Portuguese brought spices, fruits, and culinary techniques from Africa, Brazil, and Asia, which gradually integrated into our eating habits. Consequently, dishes like feijoada or seafood rice are now considered classics, even though they have roots in distant cultures. Yet, it wasn’t always easy to accept these changes. Many feared that the authenticity of Portuguese cuisine would be lost, but fortunately, the opposite happened: it became richer. Today, Portuguese and foreign chefs work side by side so that national gastronomy continues to surprise. Michelin-starred restaurants reinterpret traditional recipes using modern techniques, never forgetting the soul of the flavors. Since globalization has brought cultures closer, it’s natural that our cuisine becomes increasingly diverse. As long as respect for origins is maintained, there’s no reason to fear innovation. Therefore, the next time you taste a Portuguese dish, remember: you’re not just savoring food, but a journey through time, full of stories, encounters, and reinventions. And who knows? Perhaps that’s the magic that makes our gastronomy continue to conquer palates worldwide.
