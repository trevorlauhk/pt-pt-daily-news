English

Yesterday, Ana left home to buy a birthday cake. She wanted to go to the bakery "Doce Sabor," but she didn’t know the way. So, she decided to ask for directions on the street. First, she saw a kind elderly man walking slowly. Ana approached him and asked, "Excuse me, do you know where the bakery 'Doce Sabor' is?" The man smiled and replied, "Yes, it’s near here." Go straight ahead to the traffic light, then turn right. The bakery is next to the bank. Ana thanked him and followed the instructions. After five minutes, she reached the traffic light but didn’t see any bank. She felt a little confused. Then, she saw a girl with a dog and asked again. The girl said, "Of course! The bank is on the next street, on the left." Ana kept walking and finally found the bakery. Next time, I’ll bring a map or use my phone!

English

In recent years, Portugal has seen a significant increase in immigration—a phenomenon that, while bringing numerous opportunities, also presents complex challenges to society. It is undeniable that immigrants contribute to the economy, enrich culture, and help fill gaps in the labor market. However, successful integration does not happen by magic. It requires effective public policies, open-mindedness from citizens, and, above all, a collective effort so that everyone feels part of the same community. Many immigrants arrive in Portugal with the dream of a better life but face linguistic barriers, stifling bureaucracy, and sometimes deep-rooted prejudices. It is essential that the government invests in language courses and welcoming programs so that these people can integrate more quickly. However, the responsibility does not lie solely with institutions. Each of us, as citizens, must question our own biases and be willing to learn from differences. After all, a multicultural society only thrives when there is mutual respect. Despite some progress, such as the creation of more inclusive laws, there is still much to be done. For instance, it is concerning that many highly skilled immigrants end up accepting jobs below their qualifications simply because their diplomas are not recognized. Portugal cannot afford to waste talent, since the country’s competitiveness largely depends on innovation and diversity. Therefore, it is time to view immigration not as a problem, but as a solution. A solution that, if well-managed, can transform Portugal into a more dynamic, creative, and supportive country. May it be so, so that future generations inherit a society where diversity is seen as a strength, not a threat.
