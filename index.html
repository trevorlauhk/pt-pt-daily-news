English

Maria has a very strong toothache since last night. She cannot eat well and is very uncomfortable. So, she picks up the phone and calls the dentist's clinic near home. The receptionist answers and says: "Good morning, Sorriso clinic, how can I help you?" Maria replies: "Good morning, my name is Maria Silva and I have a terrible toothache. I want to book an appointment for today." The receptionist looks at the schedule and says: "Today we don't have free slots, but tomorrow morning we have one at ten o'clock. Is that okay?" Maria agrees and gives her full name and phone number. She feels calmer and takes a pill for the pain. Then, she prepares dinner and goes to bed early because tomorrow she needs to wake up early. The next day, she wakes up at eight, has breakfast quickly and leaves home at nine thirty. At ten o'clock sharp, she arrives at the clinic and waits in the waiting room. The dentist calls her and examines her mouth carefully. He says: "I am going to treat this tooth now. It doesn't hurt much." Maria feels relieved. After the appointment, she pays at the reception and goes home by bus. She smiles because the pain has disappeared.

A arte não é um luxo: é o coração pulsante da nossa sociedade

English

Art and culture play a fundamental role in building a fairer, more cohesive society capable of dialogue with itself. Although many citizens consider them to be merely forms of entertainment or luxuries reserved for economic elites, the truth is that they profoundly shape our collective identity. And they promote essential dialogue between different social groups, building bridges where there were once walls. It is essential that the State consistently invests in cultural spaces accessible to everyone, so that no one is excluded from this wealth that belongs to us collectively. When a child has the opportunity to paint, dance or listen to classical music, they are developing skills that go far beyond the merely artistic. Empathy, creativity and critical thinking, skills that will be useful to them throughout their entire adult life. Despite living in a digital age, where everything seems ephemeral and disposable, culture remains the invisible link that connects us to the past and projects us into the future. Therefore, it is important that each of us values local traditions, since they are the solid foundation on which we build our common and shared present. Notwithstanding the economic challenges we currently face, we cannot neglect the cultural sector. For it is there that we find inspiration to solve complex problems and to imagine innovative solutions for the community. Therefore, it is up to all of us, citizens and leaders, to ensure that art remains alive and vibrant in our streets, our schools and our hearts. So that future generations can enjoy a society that is truly more humane, creative and balanced.
