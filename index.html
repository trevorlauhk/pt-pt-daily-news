Yesterday, Ana and Pedro went to the beach with their friends. They left home early because they wanted to enjoy the whole day. The sun was strong, but the wind was also blowing a little. Ana took a big hat and sunglasses because she doesn’t like to get sunburned. Pedro forgot his towel, so he had to buy one at a shop near the beach. When they arrived, Ana ran into the water. It was cold, but she loved it! Pedro stayed on the sand playing paddleball with João. Later, they had sandwiches and juice for lunch. Ana ate two sandwiches because she was very hungry! In the afternoon, they built a huge sandcastle. Pedro said, "This is the best castle in the world!" Before leaving, they ate ice cream. On the way home, they were all tired but happy. Ana thought, "Tomorrow, I’ll go back to the beach!"

English

In recent years, Portugal has become one of the most sought-after tourist destinations in Europe. Lisbon and Porto, in particular, attract millions of visitors annually, which, at first glance, seems like excellent news for the economy. However, the impact of tourism is not as simple as it appears. While it generates jobs and wealth, there are consequences that deserve deeper reflection. One of the biggest concerns is gentrification. Since the number of tourists began to rise, housing prices have skyrocketed, making life unbearable for many local residents. It’s sad to think that, so that visitors can enjoy a comfortable stay, entire families are forced to leave the neighborhoods where they grew up. Furthermore, traditional commerce has been disappearing, replaced by souvenir shops and fast-food restaurants, which have little to do with the country’s authentic culture. Nevertheless, we cannot ignore the benefits. Tourism creates jobs and boosts the economy, especially in less developed regions. Therefore, it is essential to find a balance. Since the country depends so much on this sector, it would be unfair to ban tourists. Instead, policies should be implemented to protect residents and preserve the cultural identity of cities. In conclusion, tourism is a double-edged sword. While it brings prosperity, it can also destroy what makes Portugal unique. It is up to the government and society to ensure that growth is sustainable, so that future generations can also enjoy what we still have today.
