English

Yesterday morning I went to the gas station because my car's tank was almost empty. It was important to refuel today because tomorrow I have to go to work very far away. First, I stopped next to pump number three and turned off the engine. Then, I removed the tank cap and put the hose inside. The attendant asked: "Unleaded petrol or diesel?" I replied: "Petrol, please, fill up the tank." While the car was refueling, I looked at the price on the screen. It was very expensive this month! But there was no other choice because I needed petrol for the week. So I paid by credit card and received the receipt in my hand. I also checked the engine oil and it was fine. After refueling, I cleaned the windshield with water and soap and checked the tire pressure. I got into the car, started the engine and adjusted the rearview mirror. I left slowly and then went to lunch at a nearby restaurant. Now the tank is full and tomorrow I am going to drive happily and calmly to my work.

English

We live in an era where knowledge is renewed at a dizzying speed. Therefore, the 21st-century school cannot continue to be merely a space for transmitting content, since the information we teach today may become obsolete tomorrow. That is why it is urgent to rethink school curricula dynamically. It is essential that students develop skills that prepare them for an uncertain future, such as critical thinking, creativity, and the ability to adapt to new realities. However, many educational systems maintain traditional methods, despite the evidence showing that active learning produces significantly better results. Teachers should, therefore, act as guides and not just as holders of knowledge, so that students learn to learn autonomously and continuously. Provided there is investment in the ongoing training of teachers and in technological infrastructures, it will be possible to transform classrooms into collaborative and inclusive environments. Furthermore, education must promote values such as empathy, respect for diversity, and environmental sustainability, as these are essential to face the global challenges ahead. Although technology offers incredible tools for teaching, it in no way replaces the importance of human interaction and direct contact between peers. Thus, the role of the school becomes that of forming conscious, critical, and resilient citizens, prepared to contribute positively to society, regardless of the technological and social changes that the future may bring us. Education, after all, is the compass that guides us in this sea of uncertainty. Only in this way will we be able to prepare new generations for the challenges we cannot even imagine yet.
