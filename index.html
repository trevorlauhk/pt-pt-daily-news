English

Yesterday, Ana went to the shopping mall with her friend Rita. They wanted to buy new clothes. Ana needed a pair of trousers for work, but she didn’t know which color to choose. Rita said, "Let’s check the stores first!" In the first store, Ana tried on some black trousers. They were too tight, and she didn’t like them. Then, she saw some blue ones. They were nice but expensive. Rita showed her some grey trousers. "These are cool and cheap!" Ana tried them on and loved them. They fit perfectly! Before paying, Ana also saw a red sweater. It was pretty, but she didn’t have enough money for both things. So, she decided to buy only the trousers. Rita bought a belt and some socks. In the end, they went for coffee. Ana was happy because she found the perfect trousers.

English

In recent years, Lisbon and Porto have been facing a housing crisis that shows no signs of ending. Despite the government’s promises of solutions, many young people continue to struggle with exorbitant rents and a lack of affordable options. While it’s undeniable that the problem has worsened due to increased tourism and real estate speculation, one must ask: is anyone truly willing to change this reality? Since wages have not kept up with the cost of living, many recent graduates are forced to share tiny apartments or, in extreme cases, return to their parents’ homes. However, not everyone has that option, especially those who come from other regions of the country or abroad in order to build a career. The result? An entire generation is postponing dreams like independence, starting a family, or even buying their first home. Although there are some measures in place, such as affordable rent programs, the truth is that they are not enough to solve the problem. Nevertheless, some believe the solution lies in greater state intervention, regulating the market, and encouraging the construction of social housing. Therefore, it is crucial that citizens demand concrete answers, as only then can we ensure that cities remain places of opportunity rather than privilege. If nothing is done, Lisbon and Porto risk losing their essence: diversity, culture, and, above all, the people who make them vibrant. The question remains: will anyone hear their plea before it’s too late?
