English

Ana woke up early today. The alarm clock rang at five in the morning, but she was already excited. Today is the day of her first flight! Ana is going to visit her grandmother in Lisbon. She packed her suitcase last night. She put in clothes, a book, and a gift for her grandmother. At the airport, Ana looked at the screen. The flight to Lisbon was on time. So, she went to the check-in line. A kind lady asked: "Good morning! Do you have any luggage to check in?" Ana replied: "Yes, I have this suitcase." The lady weighed the suitcase and gave Ana the boarding pass. After that, Ana went to security control. Ana is happy because she will see her grandmother soon!

English

In recent years, the internet has become a stage where everyone can share their opinions. However, this freedom, which many defend as a pillar of democracy, has a dark side: misinformation. While it is essential that every citizen has the right to express themselves, it is equally crucial that this expression does not become a tool for manipulation. Since social media amplifies messages in seconds, the speed at which information spreads can be both a blessing and a curse. Consequently, many governments and digital platforms have debated whether or not to regulate online content. However, the issue is not simple. On the one hand, it is important that people can freely debate ideas; on the other, mechanisms are needed to prevent the spread of fake news. Some argue that any form of censorship is dangerous, as it could open the door to abuses of power. Others, however, believe that as long as the rules are transparent and fair, moderation is not only desirable but necessary. After all, freedom of expression should not be confused with the freedom to deceive. The challenge, therefore, lies in finding a balance. It is essential that platforms invest in fact-checking tools and that users develop a critical mindset. Only then can we ensure that the internet remains a space for dialogue, not disorder.
