English

Yesterday, Ana and Pedro went to the zoo. They woke up early because they wanted to see all the animals. When they arrived, the sun was shining and the birds were singing. First, they saw the lions. Pedro was a little scared, but Ana laughed and said, "Don’t be afraid!" After that, they fed the giraffes. Ana gave a big carrot to the tallest giraffe. Pedro also wanted to feed them, but the giraffe didn’t want to eat from his hand. Before lunch, they saw the monkeys. The smallest monkey stole her hat! At the end of the day, they were tired but happy. Ana asked, "Shall we come back tomorrow?" Pedro answered, "Yes! But tomorrow I’ll bring an old hat!"

Turismo em Portugal: Bênção ou Maldição para as Nossas Cidades?

English

In recent years, Portugal has become one of Europe’s most sought-after tourist destinations. While it is undeniable that tourism brings significant economic benefits, it is crucial to reflect on its long-term impact, especially in historic cities like Lisbon and Porto. On one hand, the increase in visitors has boosted the local economy, creating jobs and revitalizing neighborhoods that might otherwise be in decline. However, the rapid growth of tourism has also generated problems that cannot be ignored. The rise in housing prices, for example, has forced many residents to leave their homes, since landlords prefer to rent to tourists, who pay higher rates. Moreover, although tourism is a source of wealth, it is important that authorities take measures to ensure that the cultural identity of cities is not lost. Many locals complain that historic centers are turning into theme parks, where authenticity gives way to souvenir shops and fast-food restaurants. Therefore, it is essential to find a balance. Tourism should be encouraged, but in a sustainable way, so that future generations can continue to enjoy our cities as they are—or at least as we would like them to be. After all, what would Portugal be without its soul?
