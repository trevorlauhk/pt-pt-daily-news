English

Rui loves reading, but he doesn’t have many books at home. One day, his teacher said at school: "You can borrow books from the city library. It’s easy and free!" Rui was very happy and decided to go on Saturday. On Saturday morning, Rui put on his favorite sweater and went to the library. When he arrived, he saw a large room with many shelves full of books. He didn’t know where to start! So, he asked a kind lady who worked there: "Excuse me, how can I borrow a book?" The lady smiled and explained: "First, you have to choose a book. Then, you go to the counter with your library card." If you don’t have a card, I can make one for you now." Rui didn’t have a card, so the lady helped him fill out a form. Five minutes later, he already had the card! Rui chose three books: one about adventures, one about animals, and a joke book. At the counter, the lady scanned the books on the computer and said: "You can keep them for fifteen days." After that, you have to return them." Rui thanked her and left very happy. Now, he goes to the library every Saturday!

English

In recent years, Portugal has witnessed a significant increase in immigration, a phenomenon that, despite its challenges, also offers numerous opportunities for the country. It is undeniable that cultural diversity enriches society; however, the full integration of immigrants is not always easy. Many arrive in search of better living conditions, yet they face linguistic, bureaucratic, and even prejudicial barriers that hinder their adaptation. One of the biggest concerns is ensuring that everyone, regardless of their origin, feels welcomed and has access to the same opportunities. For this to happen, it is crucial that the government and civil society work together so that effective public policies can be implemented. For example, Portuguese language courses and targeted employment programs can make a huge difference in the lives of newcomers. Although some sectors of society resist change, it is important to remember that immigration is not a problem but a reality. Since Portugal was once a country of emigrants, it should better understand the difficulties faced by those seeking a new life. Consequently, it is essential to promote intercultural dialogue so that the values of tolerance and respect are strengthened. In the end, multicultural integration benefits not only immigrants but society as a whole. When differences are celebrated rather than feared, stronger and more cohesive communities are built. Therefore, it is up to each of us to contribute to a more inclusive and supportive Portugal.
