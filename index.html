Yesterday was Sofia’s birthday. She turned 25 and decided to have a party at home. She invited all her friends, and I went too! Before the party, I bought a nice gift: a book she really wanted. Then, I stopped by the bakery and brought a small cake to surprise Sofia. When I arrived, the house was full of people. Sofia was wearing a red dress and was very happy. Everyone sang "Happy Birthday" and she blew out the candles. Afterwards, we ate the cake and other delicious snacks. I danced with Sofia and also chatted with friends I hadn’t seen in a long time. At the end of the party, Sofia opened the presents. When she saw mine, she smiled and said, "I loved it! Thank you so much!". I was happy because she liked it. Then, I helped clean up the house and went home late, but very happy.

English

In recent years, social media has become an unavoidable part of daily life, especially for young people. While it offers numerous advantages, such as the ability to stay in touch with distant friends or access real-time information, many experts question whether its impact is truly positive. It is undeniable that platforms like Instagram or TikTok stimulate creativity and allow young people to express their opinions; however, we cannot ignore the risks they pose. One of the biggest concerns is the effect social media has on mental health. Recent studies suggest that excessive use can lead to problems like anxiety or low self-esteem, since many young people constantly compare themselves to unrealistic standards of beauty or success. Therefore, it is essential that parents and educators stay alert and promote conscious use of these tools. After all, social media is not inherently bad; it all depends on how it is used. Furthermore, it is important for young people to understand that not everything they see online reflects reality. Often, posts are carefully edited to appear perfect, which can distort the perception of real life. Nevertheless, if used in moderation and with a critical mind, social media can be an excellent tool for learning, sharing, and even mobilizing social causes. Thus, it is up to each of us to decide how to integrate them into our daily lives without letting them control us.
