Today is a very special day for Rita. She’s traveling alone for the first time! At seven in the morning, Rita wakes up early and puts on her favorite clothes. Then, she has breakfast with her mom and dad. They’re a little nervous, but happy. At eight o’clock, Rita and her parents go to the airport. On the way, Rita looks out the window and sees many cars and people. She thinks, "I’m going to miss home." When they arrive at the airport, Rita sees a big sign: "Check-in." She doesn’t know where the airline counter is, so she asks an employee. The man smiles and says, "Follow me, please." At the counter, Rita hands over her passport and ticket. The employee weighs her suitcase and says, "Your suitcase is a little heavy, but it’s okay." After check-in, Rita says goodbye to her parents. She’s nervous, but also very excited. "I’m going to visit my aunt in Lisbon!" she thinks.

English

Who hasn’t felt frustrated with a telecommunications contract that, despite promising the world, ends up trapping us with mediocre services and inflated prices? It’s true that sometimes we accept abusive clauses without questioning, since the bureaucracy seems too complex. However, it’s essential that we understand complaining isn’t just a right—it’s a civic duty—especially when companies rely on our passivity to profit at our expense. Imagine finding yourself in a situation where, after months of loyalty, you discover the package you signed up for doesn’t include what was promised. Or worse: the bill arrives with exorbitant charges without any justification. Many give up immediately, thinking it’s not worth the effort. Yet, it’s precisely in these moments that we should act. First, contact customer service and demand a clear solution. If you’re not satisfied, turn to regulatory bodies like ANACOM so that your complaint is properly reviewed. It’s important to remember that, as long as we’re informed, companies can’t ignore our rights. Therefore, before signing any contract, read all the clauses carefully. If something doesn’t seem fair, question it. And above all, don’t be afraid to complain. After all, only this way will things change—and perhaps, in the future, contracts will be more transparent and fair for everyone.
