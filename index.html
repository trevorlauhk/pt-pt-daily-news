João lives in an old building in Porto. He really likes his neighborhood, but sometimes there are problems. Last night, he heard strange noises in the street. It was two in the morning, and he was alone at home. At first, he thought it was the wind, but then he heard loud voices and breaking glass. João got scared. He didn’t know what to do. Then, he remembered his safety lessons at school. The teacher had said: "If you see something dangerous, call the police." João went to the phone and dialed 112. A calm voice answered: "Emergency, how can I help?" João explained what he heard and gave his address. Five minutes later, two police officers arrived. The next day, his neighbors thanked him. João smiled and replied: "It was nothing. It’s better to ask for help than to be afraid."

English

In recent years, Portugal has become one of the most sought-after tourist destinations in Europe. While tourism brings clear economic benefits, such as job creation and GDP growth, some question whether this phenomenon is turning the country into a kind of theme park for foreigners. It is undeniable that without the contribution of visitors, many regions would face even greater difficulties. However, the excess of tourism in cities like Lisbon or Porto has generated problems that cannot be ignored, such as rising housing prices and the loss of cultural identity. Since tourism is an essential source of revenue, it would be unfair to demonize it entirely. Nevertheless, it is crucial that the government and local authorities implement policies to ensure that benefits are distributed equitably. For example, the creation of regulated local accommodations could prevent residents from being pushed out of historic centers. Furthermore, in order to prevent tourism from becoming unsustainable, it would be wise to diversify attractions by promoting lesser-known regions, such as the Alentejo or the country’s interior. Therefore, the challenge is not to stop tourism but to manage it intelligently. If urgent measures are not taken, we risk leaving future generations with a country that is unrecognizable, where authenticity has been sacrificed in the name of profit. It is time to reflect: do we want to be a mass destination or a country that welcomes visitors without losing its soul?
