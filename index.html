Maria woke up early on the day of her trip. She was going to Lisbon to visit her grandmother. Before leaving, she checked her suitcase again: passport, ticket, mask, and a book. "Everything’s here," she thought with a smile. At the airport, Maria looked at the screens. Her flight to Lisbon was on the board. "Gate 12, at 9:30," she read. Then, she went to the check-in line. There were many people, but she wasn’t in a hurry. When she reached the agent, she said, "Good morning! Here’s my ticket." The woman smiled and asked, "Just one suitcase to check in?" Maria replied, "Yes, and this backpack is my carry-on." The agent weighed the suitcase and gave her the boarding pass. Before going through security, Maria went to the bathroom. She was a little nervous, but happy.

English

Who hasn’t ever felt trapped in a telecommunications contract that, despite promising the world, ends up being a real nightmare? It’s common to sign an agreement expecting everything to go as promised, however, that’s not always the case. Ever since providers started offering “all-inclusive” packages, many consumers have found themselves tangled in obscure clauses and unexpected charges. Therefore, it’s crucial that we know when and how to complain, so that our rights aren’t trampled. However, complaining shouldn’t be seen as an act of rebellion, but rather as a civic responsibility. Since companies depend on consumers to thrive, it’s only fair that they demand transparency and quality. Despite some people fearing retaliation, the truth is that the law protects those who report abusive practices. So, if your contract includes services that were never provided or fees that weren’t properly explained, don’t hesitate: speak up. Of course, for the complaint to be effective, some steps must be followed. First, gather all the evidence, such as bills and emails. Then, contact the provider in writing, demanding a formal response. If, even then, you don’t get a solution, turn to regulatory bodies like ANACOM. Remember: the goal isn’t just to solve your problem, but also to ensure other consumers don’t go through the same. In short, complaining is an act of citizenship. Though it may seem intimidating, it’s an essential step for the market to become fairer. After all, if we don’t demand better conditions, who will?
