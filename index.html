English

Yesterday I went to the centre of Lisbon with my friend Ana because we wanted to visit São Jorge Castle. We didn't know the city well, so we got completely lost near Rossio. We were hungry, but first we wanted to find the station. The day was beautiful and there were many people on the street. I saw a nice gentleman near an old shop and asked: 'Excuse me, where is the nearest metro station?' He smiled and said: 'It's straight ahead. You go along Rua Augusta and then turn right on the second street.' We thanked him very much and followed the directions very carefully. First we walked along the main street for ten minutes and saw many nice shops. Then we turned right and saw the big yellow theatre. Finally we found the station! We bought the tickets at the machine and got on the metro. We really enjoyed the tour of the historic centre and went back home by bus at night, very happy and tired.

Portugal turístico: entre o lucro e a identidade

B1-B2 (DEPLE/DIPLE)

English

Portugal has seen exponential growth in the tourism sector in recent years, transforming cities like Lisbon and Porto into world-renowned destinations. Although this phenomenon brings undeniable economic benefits — such as job creation and increased tax revenues —, it is essential that the Government take concrete measures so that urban centers do not turn into mere theme parks devoid of soul. Since the number of visitors continues to rise alarmingly, it becomes urgent to regulate local accommodation so that residents are not expelled from their own neighborhoods due to speculative housing prices. Despite tourism being the engine of the Portuguese economy, many citizens question whether it is worth paying such a high price for quality of life. However, one should not ignore that, provided there is adequate strategic planning, it is possible to reconcile economic development with the preservation of cultural identity. Therefore, municipal authorities must invest in sustainable infrastructure and efficient public transport, so that future generations can enjoy an authentic and welcoming country. It is crucial that we do not lose our essence just because tourism figures are appealing in the short term. However, we also cannot close our doors to those who visit us with hospitality, for that would harm a vital source of national income. Therefore, the greatest challenge consists in finding a sensible balance between welcoming tourists well and ensuring that Portuguese people live well in their own cities.
