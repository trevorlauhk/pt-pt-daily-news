Yesterday, João went to fill up his car. He stopped at the gas station near his house. When he arrived, he saw that the price of gasoline was more expensive. "Today is not my day," he thought. João got out of the car and opened the fuel tank cap. Then, he took the hose and chose the type of fuel. He wasn’t sure which was best, so he asked the attendant. "Excuse me, which is the most economical gasoline?" The attendant smiled and said: "That one there, sir." João filled up the tank and paid with his card. While he waited, he looked at his phone and saw a message from Maria. "Don’t forget to check the oil!" Before leaving, João checked the oil level and the tire pressure too. On the way home, he stopped at the supermarket to buy bread.

Contratos de Telecomunicações: Quando Reclamar Não é (Só) um Direito, mas um Dever

English

Imagine signing a telecommunications contract with the promise of fast internet and impeccable service. However, after a few months, you realize the speed is slow, calls drop constantly, and customer support seems more interested in selling new packages than solving your problems. Many consumers, despite being dissatisfied, choose not to complain, fearing the situation might worsen or that they’ll waste time on bureaucratic processes. Nevertheless, it’s crucial that we understand complaining isn’t just a right—sometimes, it’s a civic duty, since only by doing so will companies be pressured to improve. First, before filing a complaint, it’s important to gather all evidence—bills, call records, screenshots of internet speed—so that your complaint is solid. Then, you should contact customer support and calmly explain the issue. If, even then, the situation isn’t resolved, you can turn to regulatory bodies like ANACOM, which have the power to mediate conflicts and, consequently, ensure your rights are respected. Of course, no one likes wasting time on bureaucracy, but it’s essential that we don’t give up at the first hurdle. After all, if we all stay silent, companies will continue offering mediocre services, assuming customers don’t care. So, the next time your telecommunications contract doesn’t meet expectations, remember: complaining could be the first step toward real change.
