English

Yesterday, Ana went to the school library after classes. She really likes reading, but she doesn’t have many books at home. So, she decided to borrow some new books. When she arrived, the library was quiet and full of light. Ana went to the counter and said, "Good morning! I’d like to borrow some books, please." The librarian smiled and replied, "Of course! Do you have your library card?" Ana searched her backpack and found the card. "You can borrow up to five books for two weeks," she explained. Ana went to the shelves and chose three books. She returned to the counter, and the librarian stamped the books. "Here you go! Don’t forget to return them on time," she said. Ana thanked her and left happily. Now, she’ll read every day after dinner. She’s already excited to go back to the library next week!

English

In recent years, Portugal has seen a significant increase in immigration—a phenomenon that, despite its challenges, deeply enriches our society. However, true integration is not achieved merely through laws or policies, but with an openness of heart and mind. It is essential that each of us, regardless of our origin, feels welcomed and valued, so that diversity becomes a strength rather than a source of division. Many immigrants arrive in Portugal seeking better living conditions, fleeing war, poverty, or persecution. Yet, the adaptation process is not always easy. Learning the language, understanding local customs, and building a new network of contacts are essential steps, but they require time and patience. Therefore, it is crucial that host communities offer support, whether through integration programs or simple acts of solidarity in daily life. Despite initial fears, experience shows that multiculturalism brings undeniable benefits. From gastronomy to music, and through the sharing of knowledge and traditions, cultural exchange enriches us all. Since Portugal has always been a country of open doors, it is now our responsibility to ensure those doors do not close but instead become bridges. After all, the true wealth of a nation lies in its ability to unite, not divide. So, let this be an invitation to reflection: how can we, as a society, build a future where everyone has a place? Perhaps the answer lies in listening more, judging less, and, above all, believing that diversity is, and always will be, an opportunity for growth.
