Mrs. Ana lives alone in a small apartment in Lisbon. She loves her home, but last night she had a problem: a faucet broke, and water flooded the kitchen. She was very worried because she doesn’t know how to fix these things. This morning, Mrs. Ana went to the corner café and spoke with Mr. João, the owner. He said, "Ana, you need home insurance! I have one, and it’s great." If something happens, the company pays for everything. Mrs. Ana didn’t know much about insurance, but she decided to go to the agency near her home. The clerk explained, "This insurance costs only twenty euros a month." Ana thought for a moment and replied, "Twenty euros isn’t much. I’ll get the insurance today." After signing the papers, she returned home feeling calmer. Now, if another faucet breaks, she knows she’ll have help. And she even had money left for a pastel de nata! At the end of the day, Mrs. Ana wrote in her diary: "Today I learned something important." Insurance is like a friend for your home. I’ll never be afraid of small problems again."

English

In recent years, there has been a heated debate about the fate of historic buildings in our cities. While many argue that modernization is essential for progress, it is crucial that we do not forget the intangible value these constructions hold. It is not just about bricks and mortar, but rather memories, identity, and culture that, once lost, can never be recovered. For instance, imagine Porto without its iconic tiled houses or Lisbon without São Jorge Castle. It would be like erasing important pages from our history. However, preservation should not be seen as an obstacle to development, but rather as an opportunity to integrate the past into the future. Many contemporary projects show that it is possible to revitalize old spaces, provided there is political will and smart investment. Nevertheless, real estate pressure and speculation often lead to the demolition of buildings with heritage value. It is urgent that authorities implement stricter laws and tax incentives so that owners choose rehabilitation over destruction. After all, preserving heritage is not a luxury, but a civic duty. Therefore, it is up to each of us to reflect: what legacy do we want to leave for future generations? If we do not act now, we risk living in soulless cities, where the past is just a distant shadow. And that, let’s agree, would be an irreparable loss.
