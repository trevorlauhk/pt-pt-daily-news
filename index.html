English

Yesterday, my friend Ana and I went to see an apartment for rent. It was near the metro, so we were happy. When we arrived, Mr. Silva, the owner, was already waiting for us. The apartment had two rooms: a small living room and a bedroom. The kitchen was narrow, but it had everything we needed. The bathroom was white and clean, but it had no window. — I like the location — Ana said —, but I think it’s a bit dark. — That’s true — I replied —, but the price is good. Should we think about it? Mr. Silva showed us the building. On the ground floor, there was a laundry room and a small garden. He said the building was safe and the neighbors were friendly. Ana is going to talk to her parents, and I’m going to see two more apartments tomorrow. Maybe one of them will be better!

Portugal Multicultural: Desafios e Riquezas de uma Sociedade em Mudança

B1-B2 (DEPLE/DIPLE)

English

In recent years, Portugal has witnessed a significant increase in immigration, a phenomenon that, despite its challenges, deeply enriches our society. It is undeniable that cultural diversity transforms cities, bringing new colors, flavors, and perspectives. However, for this integration to be truly successful, a joint effort between immigrants and local citizens is essential. Many argue that language is the main barrier, since without mastering Portuguese, newcomers face difficulties accessing jobs, healthcare, and education. Nevertheless, it is encouraging to see more and more initiatives emerging so that everyone can learn the language and feel welcome. For example, schools and associations offer free courses, although gaps still need to be filled. Moreover, integration should not be seen merely as the responsibility of immigrants. Portuguese people must also be willing to open their minds and hearts, recognizing that while cultural differences may initially create distrust, they are, consequently, an opportunity for growth. After all, a multicultural society is not built overnight but through small daily gestures of understanding and respect. Therefore, it is crucial that we continue to promote inclusive policies and celebrate diversity, for only then can we build a future where everyone feels part of Portugal.
