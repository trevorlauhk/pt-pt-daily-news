Yesterday, Ana had a very bad headache. She didn’t want to drink tea or rest. So, she decided to go to the pharmacy near her house. The pharmacy on Rua Nova is small, but it has everything she needs. When she arrived, Ana saw a line with three people. She waited five minutes, and then it was her turn. The pharmacist asked: "How can I help you?" Ana replied: "I have a headache. I need some medicine, please." The lady showed her two types of pills and explained... Ana chose the mild one because she didn’t want to sleep. She also bought cough syrup because her brother was sick. In the end, Ana paid ten euros and thanked her. She went back home and took the medicine. After half an hour, the pain went away. Tomorrow I’ll buy throat lozenges because the weather is cold.

English

In recent years, Portugal has become one of the most sought-after tourist destinations in Europe. Cities like Lisbon and Porto are filled with visitors who admire the stunning landscapes, rich gastronomy, and warm hospitality of the Portuguese. However, despite the evident economic benefits that tourism brings, are we paying too high a price for this growth? Since the number of tourists keeps rising, many cities face serious problems, such as rising house prices and gentrification. Consequently, local residents are forced to move to more remote areas, where life is cheaper but less convenient. Nevertheless, we cannot ignore that tourism creates jobs and boosts the economy, especially in regions that would otherwise be forgotten. Although the government tries to implement measures to control the negative impact, such as limiting local accommodations, finding a balance is difficult. In order for tourism to remain a positive force, careful planning is essential, and investments must be made in infrastructure that benefits both visitors and residents. Otherwise, we risk turning our cities into theme parks, where authenticity is lost in the name of profit. Therefore, it is up to all of us to reflect: to what extent are we willing to sacrifice our quality of life in exchange for economic growth that, while important, may not be sustainable in the long run?
