Last night, I was taking a shower when the shower started making a strange noise. Then, the water wouldn’t stop running! I tried to turn off the tap, but I couldn’t. The house got all wet, and I was afraid of slipping. This morning, I called my neighbor, Mr. António, and asked: "Do you know a good plumber?" He said: "Yes, my nephew is a plumber. I’ll call him now." So, Mr. António called his nephew, João. João came to my house right away. He looked at the shower and smiled. "This is easy," he said. Then, he took his tools and worked for twenty minutes. When he finished, the water stopped running! I was very happy and thanked João. He said: "If you need anything else, just call." I paid him thirty euros and offered him a coffee. Now, the shower works perfectly, and I’m no longer afraid of taking a shower!

English

In recent years, Portugal has witnessed a significant increase in immigration—a phenomenon that, despite its challenges, deeply enriches our society. While the arrival of people from different cultures, religions, and traditions raises complex issues, it also offers unique opportunities for growth. For integration to succeed, it is essential that everyone work together to foster bonds of mutual respect and understanding. One of the Portuguese people’s greatest concerns is the preservation of national identity. However, it is important to remember that culture is not static; it evolves as new influences are absorbed. For instance, cities like Lisbon and Porto are already true cultural mosaics, where one can taste Brazilian *feijoada*, hear fado with lyrics in Creole, or celebrate Eid al-Fitr in the streets. These moments, though seemingly simple, prove that diversity does not dilute our identity but rather enriches it. Of course, not everything is perfect. Some fear that immigration may overwhelm public services or create conflicts. Yet, studies show that, as long as there are clear integration policies, the economic and social benefits far outweigh the challenges. Moreover, it is crucial that immigrants have access to language and professional training so that they can fully contribute to the country that welcomes them. Portugal, therefore, has a historic opportunity to establish itself as a model of multiculturalism in Europe. To achieve this, constant dialogue, patience, and, above all, an open heart are necessary. After all, a society is only truly strong when it can embrace its differences and turn them into a common strength.
