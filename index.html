English

Almost fifty years ago, Portugal woke up to a new dawn. April 25, 1974, was not just another day in the country's history, but the moment when the Portuguese decided it was time to end a dictatorship that had lasted for decades. Although many young people today have not lived through that era, it is essential that they understand the significance of this event, since it shaped the democracy we enjoy today. That early morning, the radios played a banned song, *Grândola, Vila Morena*, signaling the start of the revolution. The military, who until then had been seen as defenders of the regime, turned against it so that Portugal could finally breathe freedom. However, change did not happen overnight. It was necessary for the population to unite, for political parties to reorganize, and for the African colonies to gain their independence. Therefore, April 25 was not just a military revolution, but also a profound social movement. Today, when we celebrate this day, we do not do so merely to remember the past, but to reflect on the present. Although we live in a consolidated democracy, it is important that we never forget the values that brought us here: freedom, equality, and solidarity. Nevertheless, we must also ask ourselves: are we doing enough to honor these ideals? After all, a revolution does not end when a regime is overthrown; it continues in the way we live and relate to one another.
