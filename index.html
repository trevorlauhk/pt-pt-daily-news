Maria walked into a clothing store in the city center. She wanted to buy a new dress for her friend's party. The store was big and had many colors and styles. A friendly saleswoman approached and asked, "Can I help you?" Maria replied, "Yes, please. I'm looking for a dress for a party." The saleswoman showed her several dresses. One was blue, another was red, and another had flowers. Maria tried on the blue dress first. It fit well, but she thought it was a bit expensive. Then, she tried on the dress with flowers. It was cheaper and very pretty! Maria looked at herself in the mirror and smiled. The saleswoman said, "It looks great on you! We also have shoes that match." Maria decided to buy the dress and the shoes. In the end, Maria paid at the cashier and left the store happy.

Património em Perigo: Porque Devemos Preservar as Nossas Raízes de Pedra

English

There are those who say that the past is a closed chapter, yet historical and architectural heritage proves exactly the opposite. Every stone, every facade worn by time tells a story that, though silent, is more eloquent than any book. However, in the name of progress, many cities choose to demolish old buildings so that new projects can emerge. Is this really the wisest decision? Since heritage is not just a collection of bricks, but rather the collective memory of a people, its preservation should be a priority. Portugal, for example, is a country full of monuments that have witnessed centuries of history, from medieval castles to Baroque palaces. It is essential that future generations can touch these walls and feel the same admiration we feel today. Consequently, the destruction of these spaces is not only a cultural loss but also a wound to national identity. Although some argue that rehabilitation is costly, the long-term benefits undoubtedly outweigh the expenses. Cities like Lisbon and Porto show that it is possible to reconcile modernity and tradition, provided there is political will and sensitivity. Moreover, cultural tourism, which contributes so much to the economy, depends directly on the conservation of these spaces. Therefore, it is up to each of us to defend what is ours. If we do not act now, we risk future generations knowing our history only through faded photographs. And that, let us agree, would be an irreparable tragedy.
