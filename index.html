English

Mrs. Ana lives alone in a small apartment in Lisbon. She works in a clothing store and really enjoys her simple life. One day, her neighbor knocked on the door and said, "Ana, you need home insurance!" Yesterday, the pipe in my kitchen burst and ruined everything. It was a nightmare! Mrs. Ana had never thought about it. She went to the bank and spoke with Mr. Silva, who is very friendly. "This insurance costs only twenty euros a month. If something happens, we’ll help." Ana felt a little nervous, but then she thought, "Better safe than sorry." The next day, she signed the papers. Mr. Silva gave her a brochure and said, "Now everything is protected!" Ana smiled and went home feeling more at ease. That night, while watching TV, she heard a noise outside. It was just a cat, but she laughed and thought, "Now I can sleep better!"

English

In recent years, Lisbon and Porto have become dream cities for tourists and digital nomads. However, what seems like a fairy tale for some has turned into a nightmare for many residents. The housing crisis, which has lasted too long, has reached alarming proportions, and it is urgent that concrete measures be taken before it’s too late. Despite the government having implemented some policies, such as increasing public housing, the truth is that these initiatives have not been enough. House prices continue to rise, and many families are forced to leave the cities where they were born and raised. It is unacceptable that, in a developed society, people live in precarious conditions, while apartments remain empty, waiting for real estate appreciation. One of the main causes of this problem is real estate speculation. Investors buy properties at low prices, renovate them, and sell them at exorbitant values in order to make quick profits. Consequently, the supply of affordable housing has drastically decreased, leaving ordinary citizens without viable alternatives. Nevertheless, some argue that tourism also plays a crucial role in this crisis, since many property owners prefer to rent their homes short-term through platforms like Airbnb, rather than making them available for long-term rentals. To resolve this situation, collaboration between the government, local authorities, and civil society is essential. Measures such as rent price limits, the creation of more social housing, and the regulation of short-term rentals could be important steps. Additionally, investment in efficient public transport is necessary so that people can live in peripheral areas without losing quality of life. The housing crisis is not just an economic problem but also a social and human one. Until a fair and balanced solution is found, Lisbon and Porto will continue to lose their identity, becoming museum-cities where only the wealthiest can live. It’s time to act before it’s too late.
