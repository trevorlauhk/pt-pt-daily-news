English

In recent years, Portugal has stood out as one of Europe’s most committed countries to the energy transition. Despite still facing significant challenges, such as dependence on fossil fuels in some sectors, the progress is undeniable. In 2023, over 60% of the electricity consumed in the country was generated from renewable sources—a milestone that fills us with pride. However, for this success to continue, it is essential that the government and companies invest not only in technology but also in environmental education. So that the population understands the importance of adopting sustainable practices in daily life. Yet, not everything is rosy. Since wind and solar energy depend on favorable weather conditions, it is necessary to develop more efficient storage solutions. Portugal is already taking steps in this direction, with large-scale battery pilot projects, but the pace needs to accelerate. Furthermore, despite having enormous potential in wave energy, this technology has not yet reached the maturity needed for large-scale exploitation. Therefore, it is crucial to create tax incentives to attract private investment, provided that it is regulated transparently. The future of renewable energy in Portugal does not depend solely on public policies but also on each of us reducing waste. If we all do our part, we can not only ensure a healthier environment for future generations but also position the country as a global leader in the fight against climate change. After all, as the saying goes, "united we will prevail."
